# Influence at a distance: what is claimed, what the evidence shows, and why this helmet cannot do it

Influence at a distance is the claim that one person's intention, or a device, can change another person's body or mind from afar. It is tied to the God Helmet in three ways. Todd Murphy says in a lecture that a thought can be put into someone's mind. A 2003 paper from Michael Persinger's laboratory asks in its title whether its result is "a mechanism for cognitive influence at a distance". And Persinger's 1995 essay on "directly accessing every human brain" is quoted widely by people who believe they are being targeted.

This article is documentary and evidential only. It sets out what has been claimed, tested and documented, and it contains no procedure for influencing anyone. The short version: laboratory studies of distant intention report tiny effects that fade in the best-controlled work; the largest trials of distant prayer found no benefit; no public record shows a device that controls thoughts from afar; and the field of this helmet falls to household background levels about ten centimetres from a coil.

## How to read this article

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **established** | Independently replicated |
| **single-lab** | Reported by one laboratory or research group only |
| **claim** | Stated by an author, agency or vendor without independent support |
| **anecdote** | A personal account |
| **unverified** | Could not be checked against a primary source |

No form of influence at a distance is **established**. The word applies here to three other things: the null result of the large prayer trials, microwave hearing at high pulse power, and the fall of a coil's field with distance.

Government programmes are described as history only. Nothing here is guidance for observing, influencing or harming anyone.

| Family | The claim | How it has been tested |

## Mental influence: the laboratory record

### Sleep at a distance (1885–1930s)

In Le Havre in 1885–86, the physician Gibert and the psychologist Pierre Janet tried to put one hypnotic subject, "Madame B.", to sleep by willing it from half a mile or more away. Frederic Myers, who watched five days of trials, counted 19 "coincidences" in 25 attempts; Janet counted 16 in 22. Myers wrote that in one trial "it of course seemed probable that it was merely our arrival which had suggested to her that she was expected to fall asleep" (Myers, "On Telepathic Hypnotism", *Proceedings of the SPR* 4, 1886, pp. 127–137). One subject, no control series: **anecdote**.

Leonid Vasiliev repeated such work in Leningrad from the 1920s and claimed success over 1,700 km (Mishlove, *The Roots of Consciousness*, rev. ed., p. 238) (**claim**; his books were not read). He began from a material theory of telepathy, and his own experiments are said to have "proved quite the opposite" (*Encyclopedia of Occultism & Parapsychology*, 5th ed., p. 1628). Later writers still read him as proof of technology. A 1997 book quotes his remark that "a radio set was used" as "a control of the subject's condition", and presents it as "remote hypnosis using undefined techniques of radio control" (Keith, *Mind Control, World Control*, 1997) (**claim**).

### Staring and "direct mental interaction"

Between 68 % and 94 % of people say they have felt an unseen stare ([Wiseman & Schlitz 1997](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/parapsychology/2005-wiseman.pdf)). The laboratory version measures skin conductance while a distant person stares, or tries to calm or arouse, in random periods.

- **The founding series.** William Braud and Marilyn Schlitz reported 15 electrodermal experiments: 6 significant, combined z = 4.08, mean effect size r = .25 ([Braud & Schlitz 1991](https://seemj.journals.publicknowledgeproject.org/index.php/seemj/article/download/112/94/187)) (**single-lab**).
- **Meta-analyses.** Across 36 such studies the effect was d = .11 (p = .001). In the seven best studies it was d = .05 (p = .50). Fifteen staring studies gave d = .13. The authors saw "hints of an effect, but also a shortage of independent replications and theoretical concepts" ([Schmidt et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15142304/)). A later analysis of 11 attention-helping studies again gave d = .11 ([Schmidt 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22784339/)). The pooled effect remains a **claim**; we found no pre-registered replication.
- **The experimenter effect.** A sceptic and a proponent ran the same staring experiment in the same laboratory. Schlitz's receivers showed an effect (p = .04); Wiseman's did not (p = .64). The difference between the two was itself not significant (p = .17) ([Wiseman & Schlitz 1997](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/parapsychology/2005-wiseman.pdf)). Their third joint study "failed to replicate" the pattern ([Schlitz et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16848945/)).

An effect of d = .1 is a very small shift in skin conductance. None of these studies claims to place a thought, a word or an action in anyone.

### Distant healing and prayer

| Study | Design | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Astin, Harkness & Ernst 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10836918/) | Review of 23 randomised trials, 2,774 patients | 13 trials (57 %) positive; "methodologic limitations" prevent firm conclusions | review |
| [Ernst 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12778776/) | Update with 17 newer studies | Rigorous new trials "shift the weight of the evidence against" more than placebo | review |
| [Sicher et al. 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9866433/), then [Astin et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17131980/) | Distant healing in AIDS: 40 patients, then 156 | The small trial was positive; the larger one found "no significant treatment effects" | **single-lab**, not confirmed |
| [MANTRA II 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16023511/) | 748 heart patients, masked off-site prayer | No difference in the primary outcome | null |
| [STEP 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16569567/) | 1,802 bypass patients in 6 hospitals | Complications in 52 % with prayer and 51 % without. Among patients *told* they were prayed for: 59 % | null; **established** with MANTRA II |
| [Cochrane review 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19370557/) | 10 trials, 7,646 patients | Findings "equivocal", with no clear effect on death; the authors are "not convinced that further trials of this intervention should be undertaken" | review |
| [Roe, Sonnex & Roxburgh 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25457442/) | Proponents' meta-analysis of 57 human studies | r = .203, heterogeneous, tied to study quality | **claim** |

The STEP result matters twice. Prayer that patients could not be sure of did nothing measurable. Being told of it went with more complications. What people are told can affect them; that is an ordinary channel.

## What Persinger and Murphy claimed

- **Murphy.** In his lecture he says that in a certain state "you can project an intention" ([1:01:42](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=3702)) and that a thought can be put into another person's mind. His evidence is a story: over a meal a friend came to say a word, "Afghanistan", that Murphy had written down beforehand ([1:02:18](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=3738)). He calls it "a simple, almost parlor trick" himself. The word was in the news and he took part in the conversation. **Anecdote**, with no control.
- **Persinger 1995.** The paper is a theoretical essay with no new experiment. Its abstract says that stimulating the brain's "codes" by applied patterns "may require energy levels which are within the range of both geomagnetic activity and contemporary communication networks" ([Persinger 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7567396/)) (**claim**). Its key sentence calls influencing "the approximately six billion brains" a potential that is "marginally feasible", and the paper ends by asking for "continued and open discussion" ([online copy](https://www.livingplanet.be/persinger.pdf)). Online copies differ on one phrase of that sentence, so the printed wording is **unverified** ([HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)). The lab's own patterns, the paper says, were applied across the head at "less than 10 milligauss (1 microT)".
- **Persinger, Koren & Tsang 2003.** The "influence" in the title is a rise in 5–6 Hz EEG power in four siblings while the other sibling sat in the ring of coils ([abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14738355/)) (**single-lab**; see [Telepathy](EFFECT_TELEPATHY.md) and [Linked brains](EFFECT_LINKED_BRAINS.md)).

None of these sources reports an experiment in which a field or an intention made a distant person think or do a chosen thing.

## The documented history

[HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md) has the record with document links. In outline:

| Item | What the record shows | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|
| **MKULTRA**, CIA, 1953–1964 | 149 subprojects, the largest group on drugs and alcohol; 8 on hypnosis; single ones on electroshock, "harassment techniques for offensive use" and ESP. Senator Kennedy: the CIA "drugged American citizens without their knowledge or consent". The files were destroyed in 1973 ([Senate 1977](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/publications/prioject-mkultra-the-cias-program-of-research-in-behavioral-modification-1977-joint-hearing-before-us-senate/)) | Director Turner's list of subject areas names no device for influence at a distance |
| **Soviet, Czech and US "psychotronics"** | A 1975 US intelligence report said the Soviet and Czech field "has powerful potential for use as an effective weapon", and also that "observed phenomena are unstable and there is low probability of proving them in controlled tests" ([DIA 1975](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00787R000500420001-2.pdf)) (**claim**). In the US, "some military decision makers" imagined "planting thoughts in individuals without their knowledge" and "hypnotizing individuals at a distance" ([NRC 1988](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED296127.pdf), p. 171) | The same review: "Nothing approaching a scientific literature supports the claims for psychotronic weaponry" |
| **The Moscow Signal**, 1953–1970s | A microwave beam on the US Embassy, corrected to no more than 0.05 mW/cm². The leading theory was that it served eavesdropping ([NSA Archive 2022](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/intelligence-russia-programs/2022-09-13/moscow-signals-declassified-microwave)) | Primate tests: "the data do not present any evidence of a behavioral change". A 1979 Senate report found "no convincing evidence" of harm to health |
| **Microwave hearing** ([Frey 1962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13895081/)) | People hear pulsed microwaves as a "click, buzz, hiss, knock, or chirp" (**established**). It needs a quiet room and pulses of typically 500 to 5,000 mW/cm² at the head ([Elder & Chou 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14628312/); [full text](https://waves.lima-city.de/dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)) | A 1998 Army paper imagined voice transmission and mentions "one experiment" with the words one to ten ([NGIC 1998](https://archive.org/download/cia-and-ussr-research-on-electromagnetic-frequencies-collection/%5BArmy%5D%20Bioeffects%20of%20Selected%20Nonlethal%20Weapons.pdf)). It assesses a possibility and reports no fielded device. The usual source for that experiment, Justesen 1975, was not opened (**unverified**) |
| **Havana syndrome** (investigated 2018–2025) | Official assessments conflict ([HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)) | No assessment proposes a microtesla coil; the mechanisms discussed are pulsed radio-frequency energy or ultrasound |

Compare the numbers: pulses of 500 mW/cm² and more are at least 10,000 times the corrected Moscow Signal level (computed). Microwave hearing is a faint sound produced in the head by pulses from a radar-class transmitter. It is not thought control.

## The physics: how far a helmet's field reaches

A small coil driven at a few hertz to a few kilohertz does not send out a beam. Its field stays around the coil and falls roughly with the cube of the distance: ten times farther away, a thousand times weaker ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md), "What this helmet delivers"; [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md)). Computed with the project's coil model, on the axis of one coil at the hardware limit of 4.54 mA:

| Distance from the coil | Field | For comparison |
|---|---|---|
| 20 mm (cortex under the coil) | about 8 µT | Earth's static field: about 35–50 µT ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md), [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)) |
| 60 mm (deep in the head) | 0.5 µT | |
| 10 cm | 0.12 µT | Average home, mains fields: about 0.07–0.11 µT ([SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md)) |
| 30 cm | 0.005 µT | |
| 1 m | 0.00014 µT (0.14 nT) | About 300,000 times weaker than Earth's field |
| 10 m | 0.00000014 µT (0.14 pT) | |

Eight coils together can add up to eight times these values at most. Programs run at 1 µT by default, eight times lower again.

Neurons respond to the electric field that a changing magnetic field induces. Even directly under a coil, smooth signals from this helmet induce about five orders of magnitude less than the level at which neural networks start to respond ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)). A person across the room receives a field far below the background of their own home. A helmet of this kind cannot reach beyond the head it sits on, and independent double-blind tests found no effect of such fields even on the head that wears them ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)).

## Accounts of being targeted

Many people describe being followed, watched or attacked by unseen operators, and a large body of web writing says remote mind control is in routine use against ordinary citizens. These accounts deserve an accurate description.

- **What they assert.** A 2011 essay by a self-described targeted individual, "The Silent Massacre", reposted on websites devoted to the subject, says scientists developed a method "to locate, focus on, and lock onto a person's brain by remote", and speaks of "hundreds of thousands, perhaps millions of targets" (**claim**). Personal statements describe voices, sensations of being struck by energy, and coordinated stalking (**anecdote**).
- **How the God Helmet enters.** Compilations and statements on such sites cite Persinger's 1995 paper as proof that the means "already exist", and say he worked on "a Pentagon weapons project", funded "by the Navy and reportedly the NSA" (**unverified**). The record shows one Navy grant of $10,000 in 1983 and a theoretical essay ([HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)).
- **What research on these experiences found.** In 50 published accounts of "gang-stalking", 40 % described being remotely controlled. The authors estimate that up to 0.66 % of women and 0.17 % of men have such experiences at some time, and stress how serious the consequences are: people who report group stalking score higher on depression and post-traumatic symptoms than people stalked by one person ([Sheridan, James & Roth 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32268595/)).

**What the evidence supports.** Agencies did run abusive experiments on people who had not consented. Microwave hearing is real at high pulse power. The experiences are real, and so is the distress.

**What it does not support.** No tested method, mental or technical, places thoughts, words or commands in a person at a distance. The official reviews that looked found none, and the physics of weak fields rules out devices like this helmet.

**If such experiences trouble you.** Hearing a voice or feeling watched is common. About one person in ten hears a voice at some time in life ([Maijer et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28956518/)), and suspicious thoughts of one kind or another are reported by up to nearly 30 % of people ([Bebbington et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23661767/)). The causes are many: lack of sleep, fever, migraine, medicines, alcohol and drugs, sight loss, and neurological or mental-health conditions ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/hallucinations-hearing-voices/)). A doctor is the right first contact, whatever you think the cause is. Seek urgent help if a voice tells you to harm yourself or anyone else. A helmet session cannot show whether someone is being targeted, and nothing in this project treats anything.

## Trying it with this helmet

The app offers nothing for influencing another person, and it will not. The site's rule is in the first paragraph of [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md): this material is "documentary history only", it "gives no guidance for influencing or harming anyone, and nothing in the record shows that fields like this helmet's can do so". Never use the device, or this history, to persuade someone that they are being influenced.

|---|---|---|
| A telepathy trial in [Trials](#trials), with [Telepathy - sender (Persinger 2003 style, 6 x 5 min)](#programs/telepathy-sender) | Two people who both agree. The helmet commits to a hidden target before the receiver answers ([Telepathy](EFFECT_TELEPATHY.md)) | **single-lab** |
| [Shapes and colours telepathy test (two people)](#guides/shapes-colors) | A simpler committed trial | **unverified** |

| The field itself | Measure it with a phone magnetometer ([CALIBRATION.md](CALIBRATION.md)). The model predicts that the reading is lost in the background about 10 cm from a coil | computed; check it yourself |

A fair series is planned in advance and long: about 279 telepathy trials at the autoganzfeld hit rate of 32 %, and 17 to 64 real and as many sham sessions to detect a very large to medium difference ([EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md)). What these tests cannot show: a hit rate above chance is not influence, and it says nothing about the helmet unless real beats sham.

Read [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) first. The programs are not for anyone with epilepsy or a seizure history; stop on headache, nausea or distress.
