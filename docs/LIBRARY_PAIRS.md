# Library notes: two people, two rings

These are reading notes. They were taken in November 2026 while every program of the library was checked against its sources ([PRESET_AUDIT.md](PRESET_AUDIT.md)), and they are published as they were taken: one row per statement, with the page and, where it is short, the passage. Pages are PDF page numbers with the printed page in brackets, for example `p.3 [661]`. The last column says how a statement was read: `image` on the page image, `text` in the text layer only (scans confuse µT with mT and drop minus signs, so no decision rests on such a value alone), `web` on a web page. Statements about the vendor's products come from the vendor's own pages and are claims. Nothing here was measured on a helmet.

## Two-person apparatus

For each experiment: the hardware and its use as the source states it, then what one 8-coil helmet on one head can and cannot reproduce. Sources and pages are those of the table above.

### Shared memories (Persinger, Cook & Tiller 2002; Booth, Charette & Persinger 2002)

- Arrays: one 8-solenoid ring, on one person only: the "response" person who narrates (PCT02 p.2-3 [532-533]: "An eight-solenoid device (Cook, et al., 1999) was placed around the field-exposed person's head"). The other person sat in a second room with no device.
- Distance: not given in metres. Booth: "another room separated by two doors and a wall" (BOOTH02 p.2 [556]).
- Drive: one computer; nothing to synchronise.
- Field: six configurations of Table 1, 5 min each, 1.5 min no field between (PCT02 p.3 [533]); Booth: 5 min each, 15 + 15 min. Strength not stated (Booth: "weak (microTesla)"); the cited ring gave 5-10 µT next to the scalp (COOK99 p.2 [62]).
- Outcome: blind matching of the exposed person's narrative with the partner's text by 44 students (PCT02 abstract); ranking of five postcards by the exposed person (BOOTH02 p.2-3 [556-557]). Both rest on the exposed person's reports.
- One helmet: delivers the whole magnetic exposure of the experiment, since only one person was exposed. Not reproduced: the burst-firing waveform (unpublished; stand-in), and the dose profile (this helmet at uT 1 gives about 24 µT at the scalp against 5-10 µT, and falls off faster with depth).

### Siblings (Persinger, Koren & Tsang 2003)

- Arrays: one ring, on the stimulus person only, in an acoustic chamber that was a Faraday cage (SIB03 p.3-4 [879-880]). The response person had EEG electrodes, blindfold and earplugs, no ring.
- Distance: "an adjacent room" (SIB03 p.3 [879]); "either 5 m or 10 m" only in the later review (PL10 p.9 [793]).
- Drive: one computer running one batch file (SIB03 p.5 [881]: "The activation of each pattern was controlled by a single batch file").
- Field: six patterns of Table 1, 5 min each, 30 min; at most 1.5 µT within 1 cm inward of the ring, 0.5 µT at the ring centre (SIB03 p.4 [880]).
- Outcome: 8-channel EEG of the unexposed person, one 20-s sample 2.5 min into each pattern (SIB03 p.5 [881]); exit questionnaire of both.
- One helmet: delivers the whole magnetic exposure. Not reproduced: the reported 0.5 µT at the centre of the head (this helmet at uT 1: roughly 0.02 µT at 9 cm, extrapolated from the model) and the burst-firing waveform.

### Strangers after proximity (Persinger, Tsang, Booth & Koren 2008)

- Arrays: one ring, on the stimulus person only (STR08 p.4 [10]: "One subject (the stimulus person) was exposed to the circumcerebral magnetic fields while the EEG of the other person was taken").
- Distance: "an adjacent room"; no metres.
- Drive: one computer, one batch file (STR08 p.5 [11]).
- Field: six patterns of its Table 1, 5 min each; 1.5 µT within 1 cm of the ring, 0.5 µT at the centre (STR08 p.4 [10]).
- Outcome: 8-channel EEG of the unexposed person (20 s at 2.5 min of each pattern) and exit questionnaires of both (STR08 p.5 [11]).
- One helmet: as for the siblings.

### Yoked rings and light flashes (Persinger et al. 2010)

- Arrays: two identical 8-solenoid rings, one on each person (YOK10 p.2 [232]).
- Distance: 5 m in Experiment 1, 10 m in Experiment 2 (YOK10 p.2-3 [232-233]); 15 m in the review (PL10 p.11 [795]).
- Drive: both rings ran the same configuration "at any given time" (YOK10 Fig. 1 caption, p.2 [232]); the review says "synchronized by being connected to the same computer" (PL10 p.11 [795]). No timing accuracy is given in either. For the same two-ring equipment: each ring has its own commutator, both controlled by one 286 computer, port time about 200 µs, 1-ms time base (DP12 p.3 [74]; DOT11 p.9 [85]).
- Field: six rotations, 5 min each (200-20, 100-10, 20-2, 200+20, 100+10, 20+2); waveform and strength not stated.
- Outcome: EEG of the response person only (8 channels in Experiments 1-2, 19 in Experiment 3), 20 or 30 s before and during the light.
- One helmet: delivers one of the two exposures. Cannot reproduce: the second ring, and the sample-by-sample identity of the two fields that a single computer gave. Two helmets started by hand run the same program with an unknown offset of the order of the reaction time or more, while one rotation lasts 0.52 s (100+10) or 1.08 s (100-10), so the two rings would be at different coils at any instant.

### Shared rings and photon counts (Dotta, Buckner, Lafrenie & Persinger 2011)

- Arrays: two identical rings of 8 solenoids (55-60 cm circumference), one on each person.
- Distance: 10 m, different rooms (DOT11 p.9 [85]).
- Drive: "connected to the same 286 Computer" in a third room; "always based upon actual 1 ms intervals" (DOT11 p.9 [85]). No further accuracy figure.
- Field: Thomas and burst-firing, 6 min each, on 20+2 (104 ms per turn) or 20-2 (216 ms per turn); 1-5 µT inside each solenoid, 1-2 µT average "within the cross-sectional area" (DOT11 p.10 [86]).
- Outcome: photon counts from a photomultiplier tube about 15 cm from the right side of the head of the person in the dark, once per second (DOT11 p.7 [83], p.10 [86]); the light went to the other person. Light and counts were aligned by "synchronizing clocks of the equipment in both rooms".
- One helmet: one of the two exposures. Cannot reproduce: the second ring and its lock to the first; the outcome needs a photomultiplier in a light-tight room.

### Gamer pairs (Lehman 2016, thesis chapter 4)

- Arrays: two rings of 8 solenoids, one on each player; 10 m apart; "connected to the same Intel 286 computer" (LEH16 p.65 [56]).
- Session start "synchronized between players, marked with a stopwatch" (LEH16 p.67 [58]): this concerns the game runs, the fields came from the one computer.
- Field: 22 min: 4 min none, 6 min "accelerating pattern", 8 min "decelerating pattern", 4 min none; 1-5 µT inside each solenoid.
- Outcome: game run times of both players, called out and written down by assistants.

### Halo pairs, 300 km (Burke, Gauthier, Rouleau & Persinger 2013)

- Arrays: no solenoid ring. One toroid ("halo") per person: 225 turns on a 25.4-cm ring, a single winding (BUR13 p.3 [37]). Both the stimulus and the response person wore one.
- Distance: 300 km (Timmins and Sudbury); later papers say 400 km (SCO15 p.10 [667], text).
- Drive: one Arduino Uno per site ("The shape of the magnetic field was modulated using an Arduino Uno microcontroller", BUR13 p.3 [37]; "generated from the programs within laptop computers to Arduino circuits", SCO15 p.11 [668], text). Synchronisation: only "Communication was maintained by cell phone between experimenters in order to ensure accuracy of time stamps on the EEG data". No statement on starting the two fields together, no accuracy.
- Field: "230 amplitude-modulated points (333 Hz, 1.45 Hz cycle)", about 1 µT average (place not stated), "phase modulated in 2 ms increments (20 ms delay to 8 ms delay)". Six 30-s stimulus trains with 30-s pauses per sequence; one sequence with field, one without.
- Outcome: sLORETA from 19-channel EEG of the response persons only.
- One helmet: one of the two exposures, and that only as an analogue. Outside its winding a toroid of this kind acts roughly as one turn of wire round the head (reader's calculation: about 5 µT per ampere at the ring centre, along the body axis); eight small coils pointing into the head give local radial fields instead, and a rotation from coil to coil that the halo did not have.

### Trans-Atlantic pairs (Scott et al. 2015)

- Arrays: one toroid per person (same construction), held by an elastic cap (SCO15 p.14 [671]).
- Distance: 6341 km (Sudbury-Berlin) and 6019 km (Sudbury-Madrid) (SCO15 p.13 [670], Table 3).
- Drive: two sets of equipment: "identical microcontrollers receiving synchronized signal-generating procedures from separate laptop computers" (SCO15 p.14 [671]). Synchronisation: stopwatches set "to the second" over Skype, start about 5 min later, "Timing was precise (within 1 s)" (SCO15 p.12 [669]); the primer began when the USB cable was plugged in at minute 11 and the effector was "initiated manually" after 360 s (SCO15 p.17 [674]); "The onsets of the two fields were synchronized to the nearest 1 s" (RKP15 p.3 [710]). One pulse cycle lasts 0.12 to 0.2 s, so the two pulse trains had no defined phase relation.
- Field: 3-ms 5-V pulses; primer 7 pulses with gaps 20 to 32 ms for 6 min; effector with gaps from 20 ms falling by 2 ms for 14 min; 30 nT average (place not stated; 30 nT at the toroid centre for the same equipment, ROU14 p.3 [46]).
- Outcome: 19-channel EEG of both people, compared between the two; mood scale before and after.
- One helmet: one of the two exposures, as an analogue (radial coils instead of a ring winding). Because the original was only synchronised to about 1 s, two helmets started by hand within 1 s reproduce its degree of synchrony.

### Harribance sessions (Roll et al. 2002; Persinger 2010)

- One person. Four solenoids on each side over the temporoparietal region (two strapped containers in the first report, a helmet in the retelling), driven as left-right pairs, 0.5 s per pair; later series 5 s per pair.
- Field 0.5-1.5 µT at the depth of the cortical surface (ROLL02 p.8 [203]).
- Outcome: his own button presses (1997); proportion of alpha rhythm and rated accuracy of his statements (later series).

## Places on the head that a source states

No source in this family gives 10-20 site names, MNI or Talairach coordinates, or distances from landmarks for a coil. What they give:

- Ring, coil 1 "over the left prefrontal region", coils 1-4 over the left hemisphere and 5-8 over the right, 45° apart, "along the orbitotemporal plane" (COOK99 p.2 [62]).
- Ring "in the horizontal plane just above the ears and approximate to the epicanthal-meatal line"; rotation "began over the left prefrontal region and ended in the right prefrontal region" (RICH02 p.3 [673]).
- Ring "along the plane supported by a line between the midforehead and above the ears" (SWANN02 p.4 [930]).
- Coil 1 "over the left frontal region", coil 2 "over the left frontal-temporal interface", coil 8 "over the right prefrontal region" (PL10 p.9 [793]).
- Order of regions round the ring: left prefrontal, left posterior frontal, left temporal, left occipital, right occipital, then right temporal to right prefrontal (STR08 p.10 [16]).
- Ring circumference 55-60 cm (DOT11 p.9 [85]; LEH16 p.65 [56]); about 60 cm (DP12 p.3 [74]).
- Halo "such that the rostral-caudal plane was just above the dorsal ear" (BUR13 p.3 [37]); toroid "over the head (level of the temporal lobes)" (SCO15 p.10 [667], text).
- Harribance: containers "attached to each temporoparietal region of the scalp" (ROLL02 p.8 [203]); four pairs "arranged in an elliptical pattern at the level of temporoparietal region" (HAR10 p.5 [448]); containers 10 cm by 7 cm (BP96 p.4, text).
- EEG sites of the outcome measures (not coil sites): F7, F8, T3, T4, P3, P4, O1, O2 (SIB03 p.3 [879]; STR08 p.4 [10]; YOK10 p.2 [232]); 19-channel 10-20 caps (BUR13 p.3 [37]; SCO15 p.12 [669]; YOK10 p.3 [233]).
- Photomultiplier "~0.15 m along the right side of the 'response' subjects' heads at the level of the temporal lobes" (DOT11 p.10 [86]).
