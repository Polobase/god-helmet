## Shiva as described

### Coils and channels

- Consumer Shiva: 16 coils on 8 channels. "Its 8 channels circulate magnetic signals around your head through 16 magnetic coils." "Shiva uses 8 pairs of magnetic coils to deliver its stimulation." (GH/shiva/understanding_shiva_neural.htm). Both coils of a pair carry the same channel number and sit side by side on the band (pictures like_polarities.jpg and shiva_head3.jpg). The pair is recommended with like polarities, as in the lab.
- The lab ring it copies has the same count: "Each pair of solenoids of the eight containers (16 solenoids in total)" (Tsang 2004 p.4 [1186]); "Each of the eight canisters contained two solenoids" (Richards 2002 p.3 [673]). The Swann paper and Cook 1999 speak of "eight solenoids"; Cook's Fig. 1 caption says "eight solenoid pairs".
- Lab variant: 64 solenoids on 8 channels. "The 64-solenoids were distributed equally around the cerebrum in columns of 8 separated by 45 deg." (Saroka 2010 p.4 [469]); "each of the 8 strips of 8 solenoids from the apex to the most lateral extent of the skull was activated for specific durations in a counterclockwise direction" (p.5 [470]). So each channel is one meridian strip from the vertex down to the hat brim; all eight solenoids of a strip get the same signal.

### How they are driven

- Consumer and 64-solenoid version alike: four USB stereo sound devices play wave files, two channels each (GH/shiva/shiva_demo/test_shiva_player.htm; Saroka 2010 p.4 [469]; Collins thesis p.38 [29]: "Each USB audio device delivered the signal to two successive solenoid columns"). No amplifier or power supply is described. Level is set by the Windows volume (50 % to 92 %).
- Lab ring: one 8-bit DAC whose output a computer-controlled 1-of-8 selector passes to one solenoid pair at a time (Cook 1999 p.2 [62], Fig. 1). The waveform runs on while the selector moves, so each position receives a slice of it ("having the signal 'chopped' into different lengths as they are played through the 8 channels", GH experiment_signals.htm).

### Ring geometry and placement rules

- Vendor rules, complete: "The eight pairs of coils are arranged so that there are no coils above the nose and none above the bony ridge at the back of the head. There are no coils directly above the ears. Place the coils evenly around your head." (locations.htm); "with the first coil in the series of eight over the left frontal area" (shiva_safety.htm, rule 6); "8 pairs of coils, equally spaced around the head" (shiva_coil_setup.htm); "Don't experiment with new arrangements for the coils." (rule 7). The vendor gives no 10-20 names, no distances and no height above the ears.
- Lab ring: "in the horizontal plane just above the ears and approximate to the epicanthal-meatal line", beginning "over the left prefrontal region" and ending "in the right prefrontal region" (Richards 2002 p.3 [673]); "every 45 deg" (Cook 1999 p.1 [61]); "the plane supported by a line between the midforehead and above the ears" (Swann 2002 p.4 [930]).

### Signals specified as numbers

- Shiva amygdaloid signal: 289 points, 3 ms per point (867 ms), values 0-256 mapped to -5...+5 V; 4000 ms between cycles in the "normal speed" session (Saroka 2010 p.5 [470]; Collins thesis p.40 [31]). The point values are not published.
- Lab burst-firing file: 230 points (Ryczko 2002 p.1 [592]). Lab frequency-modulated file: 849 points (Tsang 2004 p.5 [1187]).
- Vendor, for the Shakti versions of the same files: 3-ms points and about 4 s of silence; high-speed = 1-ms points; hippocampal signal's spectral peak 5.4 Hz; about 9.6 s of field per minute; 16-bit wave files.
- Nothing numeric is published for the hippocampal (LTP), caudate, septal, ERP, modulated 40 Hz or chirp files, beyond "40 Hz" and "very low frequencies".

### Field strength claims and where measured

- Consumer Shiva: no number ("about as strong as the ones from a telephone handset or a hair dryer").
- Same coils sold as Shakti: 10 mG "when measured from one coil" (sensor distance not given); "10 to 20 milligauss" (place not given). Independent: 0.1-1.0 uT rms with a handheld meter, place not given (Gendle 2012 p.4-5 [220-221]).
- Shiva software in the lab: "about 10 mG (1 microTesla)" "within the volume occupied by the subjects' heads" (Collins thesis p.40 [31]); "estimated intracerebral strength of 1 uT" (Saroka 2010 p.4 [469]); 1-2 uT "in the center of the cap" by "a power meter" for the 8-coil God Helmet layout (Collins thesis p.69 [60]). None of the three says how the value was obtained.
- Lab ring: 5-10 uT at the scalp (Richards 2002, Cook 1999), 0.5-1.5 uT near the scalp (Tsang 2004), 2-5 uT without place (Swann 2002). Booth 2008 cites Tsang 2004 for 5-10 uT; Tsang 2004 prints 0.5-1.5 uT.

## Places on the head that a source states

No source of this family gives 10-20 site names, MNI or Talairach coordinates, or distances from landmarks for a coil. What they give:

- Lab ring: "horizontal plane just above the ears and approximate to the epicanthal-meatal line"; first position left prefrontal, last right prefrontal (Richards 2002 p.3 [673]).
- Lab ring: "every 45 deg" "at the level of the temporoorbital plane"; "solenoids 1-4 ... over the left hemisphere and solenoids 5-8 ... over the right hemisphere" (Cook 1999 p.1-2 [61-62]).
- Lab ring: "the plane supported by a line between the midforehead and above the ears" (Swann 2002 p.4 [930]); "the first solenoid was over the left frontal region and the eight solenoid was over the right prefrontal region" (Tsang 2004 p.4 [1186]).
- Consumer Shiva: none over the nose, the inion ridge or directly over the ears; even spacing; first coil "over the left frontal area" (locations.htm; shiva_safety.htm).
- 64-solenoid hat: 8 columns 45 degrees apart, each from "the apex to the most lateral extent of the skull" (Saroka 2010 p.4-5 [469-470]).
- 8-coil Shiva God Helmet: a square of four solenoids over each temporal lobe, "separated 5 cm in the vertical plane and 8 cm in the horizontal plane", on a baseball cap, position adjusted to head size (Collins thesis p.69 [60]).
- EEG sites (not coil sites): F7, F8, T3, T4, P3, P4, O1, O2 (Saroka 2010 p.5 [470]; Richards 2002 p.4 [674]).
