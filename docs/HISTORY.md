# History: military, intelligence and institutional involvement

Weak magnetic fields, remote viewing and "consciousness technology" have a long institutional history. The US Navy studied ELF fields as a safety question. US intelligence agencies paid for psychic research from 1972 to 1995. Health and standards bodies wrote the exposure limits this project uses. This document sets out what the records show, by institution and roughly in date order.

It is documentary history only: what was studied, funded, claimed and found. It gives no guidance for influencing or harming anyone, and nothing in the record shows that fields like this helmet's can do so. The science of the God Helmet is in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md). Exposure limits and the device's safeguards are in [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md).

## How to read this document

**Documented** means a primary record exists: a declassified file, an official report, a patent or a paper. It says nothing about whether the content of the record is true. What the records claim carries the project's evidence words:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **established** | Independently replicated |
| **single-lab** | Reported by one laboratory or research group only |
| **claim** | Stated by an author, agency or vendor without independent support |
| **anecdote** | A personal account |
| **unverified** | Could not be checked against a primary source |

Most CIA files are linked through mirrors (The Black Vault, the Internet Archive, the National Security Archive). The IDs that start with "CIA-RDP" identify the originals in the CIA's CREST reading room.

## ELF research and the Navy's antenna (1950s–1997)

### The submarine antenna: Sanguine, Seafarer, ELF

The Navy began research on ELF radio for submarines in the late 1950s. It finished a test antenna near Clam Lake, Wisconsin, in 1969. Clam Lake was fully working in 1985, and a second site at Republic, Michigan, in 1989. The signal was modulated between 72 and 80 Hz, with 76 Hz the most prominent ([NRC 1997](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/5410/an-evaluation-of-the-us-navys-extremely-low-frequency-submarine-communications-ecological-monitoring-program)).

At the Navy's request, the National Academy of Sciences set up a Committee on Biosphere Effects of ELF Radiation in 1976. Its 1977 report covers "Seafarer (formerly Sanguine)" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/R3.gif)).
- **Fields.** The Navy specified at most about 0.1 V/m and 0.37 G (37 µT) directly over the grid cables ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/35.gif)). For the Michigan proposal the report gives 0.11 G (11 µT) at the surface over a cable and about 0.002 G (0.2 µT) 100 m away. Earth's field is about 0.5 G (50 µT) ([NAS 1977](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/20341/chapter/1)).
- **Conclusion.** The fields "will not cause a significant and adverse biologic disturbance, except in the event of electric shock". The committee added that "the possibility of adverse effects cannot be fully ruled out" and asked for long-term monitoring if the system was built ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/51.gif)).
- **Nerves and behaviour.** Reported effects "occur only at field strengths well above those expected even in the immediate vicinity of the antenna" ([NAS 1977](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/20341/chapter/1)).

Two later reviews followed.
- In 1985 the American Institute of Biological Sciences reviewed the newer literature and "recommended continued ecological research" ([NRC 1997](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/5410/gif/15.gif)). Its reported finding that adverse effects were "unlikely" is known only from an abstract (**unverified**) ([AIBS 1985](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA152731.pdf)).
- The National Research Council reviewed the Navy's ecological monitoring in 1997, under a Defense Department contract. It found "no evidence that exposure to ELF EMFs had obvious adverse ecological effects". It also criticised low statistical power and pseudoreplication, said small possible effects were "too readily dismissed", and asked for a reanalysis by a body independent of the Defense Department ([NRC 1997](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/5410/an-evaluation-of-the-us-navys-extremely-low-frequency-submarine-communications-ecological-monitoring-program)).

### Navy laboratory studies at Pensacola

The Naval Aerospace Medical Research Laboratory (NAMRL) in Pensacola ran most of the Navy's human and primate ELF studies. The question of blood-fat changes was discussed at a meeting on 6–7 December 1973 "sponsored by the U.S. Navy Bureau of Medicine and Surgery" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/41.gif)). The original reports sit in DTIC; the table uses the reviews.

| Study | Exposure | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beischer et al. 1973, men | 1 G (100 µT), 45 Hz, about 10 or 22.5 h | Raised blood triglycerides reported. The NAS found the design deficient (diet not controlled, groups not matched): "this report does not support an effect". Beischer himself saw no effects "that could definitely be linked with the magnetic field" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/195.gif)) | **single-lab**, rejected |
| Beischer & Brehl, 140 mice | 1 G, 45 Hz, 24 h | "no statistically significant difference" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/195.gif)) | **single-lab** |
| Grissett & Kupper, monkeys | 2 G (200 µT), 72–80 Hz, more than a year | "no significant difference" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/195.gif)) | **single-lab** |
| Grissett & de Lorge, squirrel monkeys | 10 G (1 mT), 45 Hz, 42 days, 1 h a day | Reaction time: "No effects were seen" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/257.gif)) | **single-lab** |
| de Lorge, rhesus monkeys | 8.2–9.3 G, 15 and 45 Hz | Interpreted as "equivocal" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/259.gif)) | **single-lab** |
| Gibson & Moroney 1974, men | 1 G, 45 Hz, 24 h | Short-term memory and addition "altered" ([Marino & Becker 1977](https://andrewamarino.com/PDFs/F038-PhysiolChemPhys1977.pdf)) | **single-lab**, "preliminary"; primary text **unverified** |

The NAS also judged a health study of staff at the Wisconsin test site "deficient in many respects" ([NAS 1977](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/books/20341/gif/41.gif)).

### Academic ELF research with defence money

- **Delgado.** His 1968 study of the "stimoceiver", a radio-controlled brain implant in patients, credits the US Air Force 6571st Aeromedical Research Laboratory, the US Public Health Service and the Office of Naval Research ([Delgado 1968](https://sites.google.com/site/mcrais1/intracerebral)). Delgado acknowledged Navy and Air Force support and denied any CIA connection ([Blackwell 2014](https://inhn.org/legacy/IMAGES/CASE_STUDY_DELGADO.PDF)); claims of CIA funding are **unverified**. His 1982 Madrid study exposed chick embryos to 0.12–12 µT at 10–1000 Hz and found that "100 Hz/1.2 µT had the most consistent and powerful inhibitory effect on embryogenesis" (**single-lab**) ([Delgado 1982](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1167891/)).
- **Operation Henhouse.** Six laboratories ran a related chick-embryo test with a different, pulsed protocol: 1 µT peak, 500 µs pulses, 100 per second, 48 h. The Office of Naval Research was one of six funders. Two of six labs found significantly more abnormalities, and the pooled data were significant ([NRC 1993](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/2046/chapter/7)). The results differed sharply between labs, so the effect is not **established**.
- **Adey.** Bawin and Adey's 1976 paper on calcium efflux from brain tissue under weak ELF electric fields thanks "The Naval Electronic Systems Command under an Office of Naval Research Contract", the Air Force Office of Scientific Research and the Public Health Service. They reported decreases of up to 12–15 % at 6 and 16 Hz (**single-lab**) ([Bawin & Adey 1976](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC430435/)).
- **Adey's letter in the CIA files.** In 1973 Adey wrote to Robert P. Gow, president of the company Develco, that "these preliminary studies indicate a feasibility to manipulate and control brain states by modification of the environmental electromagnetic 'atmosphere'" ([Adey 1973](https://archive.org/download/cia-and-ussr-research-on-electromagnetic-frequencies-collection/%5BCIA%5D%201973%2C%2005%2030%20WR%20Adey%20-%20LETTER%20TO%20MR.%20ROBERT%20P.%20GOW%20FROM%20W.%20ROSS%20ADEY%2C%20MD.pdf)). A month earlier Gow had written that the intelligence community should look "definitively" at "the reported communication phenomenon associated with human biology" ([Gow 1973](https://archive.org/download/cia-and-ussr-research-on-electromagnetic-frequencies-collection/%5BCIA%5D%201973%2C%2004%2026%20Robert%20P%20Gow%20-%20NOTE%20PER%20%5BSanitized%5D.pdf)). Both letters are in the CIA's STAR GATE collection. The capability is Adey's **claim**; the record shows only that the idea reached an intelligence file.

## Microwaves: the Moscow Signal and microwave hearing (1953–1979)

From 1953, US technicians detected a microwave beam aimed at the US Embassy in Moscow ([NSA Archive 2022](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/intelligence-russia-programs/2022-09-13/moscow-signals-declassified-microwave)).
- **Project PANDORA.** ARPA studied possible effects from 1965 until March 1970, at a cost of $4,615,000. A sub-project called BIZARRE tested primates.
- **Exposure.** A first estimate of 0.5–1 mW/cm² was corrected to "not over 50 microwatts/cm2", about 2 µW/cm² at its average high.
- **Findings.** ARPA reported "no overt primate performance degradation" in 1967. A 1971 RAND panel criticised the experimental design. PANDORA meetings in 1969 discussed human experiments. A 1979 Senate report found "no convincing evidence" that the exposure harmed health.

All of the above is from the declassified files, which also show the signal still active in 1976 ([NSA Archive 2022](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/intelligence-russia-programs/2022-09-13/moscow-signals-declassified-microwave)).

In 1962 Allan Frey reported that people hear pulsed radio-frequency energy as sound ([Frey 1962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13895081/)). The effect itself is **established**; a later review supports a thermoelastic mechanism ([Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)). This "Frey effect" is radio-frequency, not ELF, and it returns in the Havana debate below.

### Soviet and Russian items with primary records

- **Kaznacheev's "mirror" experiments.** Kaznacheev's group reported that UV-stressed cell cultures damaged neighbouring cultures through optical contact (**single-lab**; no independent replication found) ([Kaznacheev 1979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/454827/)).
- **LIDA.** US patent 3,773,049 (Rabichev et al., Kishinev, 1973) covers a therapy device that pulses heat, light, sound and VHF radio together for neuroses, insomnia and hypertension ([Rabichev 1973](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3773049A/en)). Claims that it was used as a weapon are **unverified**.
- **The "Woodpecker".** From July 1976 to December 1989 a Soviet transmitter pulsed at 10 Hz (also 16 and 20 Hz) in the 7–19 MHz band. It was the Duga over-the-horizon radar; mind-control readings of it are unsupported ([HF Underground](https://hfunderground.com/wiki/index.php/Duga_Radar_%28Russian_Woodpecker%29)).
- **Military unit 10003.** According to *Rossiyskaya Gazeta*, General Staff chief Mikhail Moiseyev proposed the unit, Colonel Alexei Savin commanded it, and it was wound up at the end of 2003. Its claimed successes come from insiders and are **claims** ([RG 2009](https://rg.ru/2009/12/30/taina.html)). In 1999 the Russian Academy of Sciences' commission against pseudoscience asked for review of military projects on "torsion fields" and similar topics, "in particular" those of unit 10003 ([Kruglyakov 1999](http://www.philosophy.nsc.ru/journals/philscience/5_99/10_KRUG.htm)). That report shows the unit existed, not that anything worked. A 2015 Russian book on "psi wars" by Rubel, Savin and Ratnikov lists Edwin May, who had led the SAIC research, and the remote viewer Joseph McMoneagle among its co-authors ([RG 2015](https://rg.ru/2015/10/15/specslujbi.html)).
- **Decrees and budgets.** A 2013 overview describes a 1986 Central Committee decree on "management of living objects", a 1989 state decree founding a "Center of unconventional technologies" and a budget of "500 million rubles". Its sources are press articles, popular books and decree images from a 2009 web publication; the 1986 text "is not in the public domain" (**unverified**) ([Kernbach 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1148)).

## US psi and remote-viewing programmes (1952–1995)

### Early interest (1952–1965)

- In 1952 Andrija Puharich lectured the Defense Department on "the possible usefulness of extrasensory perception in psychological warfare" ([Kress 1977](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/remoteviewing/stargate/STARGATE%20%2311%20549/Part0005/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200030040-0.TXT)).
- In 1977 the Director of Central Intelligence, Admiral Turner, told the Senate that one of the 149 MKULTRA subprojects covered "analysis of extrasensory perception" ([Senate 1977](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/publications/prioject-mkultra-the-cias-program-of-research-in-behavioral-modification-1977-joint-hearing-before-us-senate/)).
- "Under the auspices of Project ULTRA", Stephen Abrams of Oxford's Parapsychological Laboratory wrote a review (draft dated 14 December 1965) claiming that "ESP was demonstrated but not understood or controllable" ([Kress 1977](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/remoteviewing/stargate/STARGATE%20%2311%20549/Part0005/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200030040-0.TXT)). A Wellcome Collection file labelled "MKULTRA subproject 136" holds Abrams's own correspondence, not CIA records ([Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/nvftmhd9)).

### CIA and SRI (1972–1977)

The CIA's own history comes from Kenneth Kress, its project officer ([Kress 1977](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/remoteviewing/stargate/STARGATE%20%2311%20549/Part0005/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200030040-0.TXT)):
- In April 1972 Russell Targ of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI) met CIA officers and supplied films of Soviet psychokinesis.
- An $874 demonstration with an SRI subject in August 1972 grew into a $50,000 effort by October 1972.
- Pat Price joined the work in 1973. Price's remote viewing of a Soviet site at Semipalatinsk was "at best inconclusive" as proof.
- Price died of a heart attack and "the program stopped". After that there were "no further CIA-sponsored intelligence collection tests".

Harold Puthoff's account adds that Ingo Swann, an artist, spent a week at SRI in June 1972. It gives the first contract as an eight-month, $49,909 "Biofield Measurements Program", and dates the Semipalatinsk session to July 1974 ([Puthoff 1996](https://newdualism.org/papers/H.Puthoff/CIA-Initiated%20Remote%20Viewing%20At%20Stanford%20Research%20Institute.htm)). In 1972–73 Swann and Price took part in "Scanate (scanning by coordinate)": remote viewing of sites given as geographic coordinates. Its results are programme accounts (**claim**), and the judging was later criticised (see below) ([Puthoff 1996](https://newdualism.org/papers/H.Puthoff/CIA-Initiated%20Remote%20Viewing%20At%20Stanford%20Research%20Institute.htm)). The AIR review states that the CIA programme ran from 1972 to 1977 ([AIR 1995](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4.pdf)).

### Coordinate Remote Viewing

Coordinate Remote Viewing (CRV) is a six-stage written procedure. The DIA briefing says CRV training began in mid-1982 with SRI and was "developed by Ingo Swann, an SRI subcontractor". It splits the "detection and decoding of psychic impressions" into Stages 1 to 6, starting from geographic coordinates ([DIA SUN STREAK briefing](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB534-DIA-Declassified-Sourcebook/documents/DIA-21.pdf)).

### The Geophysical Effects Study (1983–1984)

For INSCOM, SRI and the Time Research Institute compared scored remote-viewing sessions from May 1979 to August 1984 with solar, geomagnetic and ionospheric data, and with local ELF readings at 19 frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz. The study ran under the CENTER LANE Special Access Program ([Adams & Puthoff 1984](https://documents2.theblackvault.com/documents/cia/stargate/STARGATE%20%234%20176/Part0003/CIA-RDP96-00788R001800270001-4.pdf)).
- It reported "significant correlations" and a "possible relationship" with ELF between 10 and 30 Hz.
- It suggested uses in forecasting performance and in "defensive countermeasures".
- It cited Persinger's geophysical papers of 1973, 1975 and 1983 as part of its rationale.
- The authors called it a pilot study.

A 1987 paper by Hubbard and May, from inside the programme, criticised its use of global rather than local indices ([Luke 2014](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/17051/7/17051%20LUKE_Local_Geomagnetic_Activity_2014.pdf)). No independent replication is known (**single-lab**).

### Early critiques

- In *Nature*, Marks and Kammann reported that their own repeat experiments "do not verify" SRI's conclusions ([Marks & Kammann 1978](https://www.nature.com/articles/274680a0.pdf)). Tart, Puthoff and Targ replied in *Nature* 284:191 (1980).

## The 1995 AIR evaluation: Utts versus Hyman

In 1995 the CIA asked the American Institutes for Research (AIR) to evaluate the programme. The report, by Mumford, Rose and Goslin, is dated 29 September 1995 ([AIR 1995](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4.pdf)).
- **Laboratory.** Hits occurred more often than chance, but the cause was unclear. The same viewers, judge and targets were reused, and agreement between judges was not checked.
- **Operations.** End users found the reports "inconsistent, inaccurate with regard to specifics". "In no case had the information provided ever been used to guide intelligence operations." "Continued use of remote viewing in intelligence gathering operations is not warranted."
- **Scores.** By 1 May 1995 three viewers had answered 40 tasks from five organisations. Accuracy clustered at 2–3 on a 6-point scale where 1 is best; value clustered at 3–4 on a 5-point scale, with no scores of 1.

The report included two outside reviews ([AIR 1995](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4.pdf)):

| | Jessica Utts | Ray Hyman |
|---|---|---|
| Verdict | Psychic functioning is "well established" ([Utts 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/air)) | Effects are real but a paranormal cause is not shown ([Hyman 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/hyman.html)) |
| Data | Effect size 0.209 over 770 SRI sessions and 0.230 over 455 SAIC sessions | The SAIC effects are "too large and consistent to be dismissed as statistical flukes" |
| Objections | — | One person, Ed May, was "the sole judge in all the free response experiments"; viewers and targets were reused; existence needs boundary conditions |

The laboratory statistics come from essentially one research group (**single-lab**). The operational value is a **claim** the review did not support. Edwin May disputed the review in 1996 ([May 1996](https://sydney.theosophicalsociety.org.au/union_index/entries/53570)).

SAIC also looked for brain signatures of remote viewing. A magnetoencephalography (MEG) study "would not be able to detect a signal even if it existed", and an EEG study found "No significant change in alpha" ([AIR 1995](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4.pdf)). No validated brain marker of remote viewing exists; see [BRAIN_VIEW.md](BRAIN_VIEW.md).

The CIA declassified SRI documents in July 1995 ([Puthoff 1996](https://newdualism.org/papers/H.Puthoff/CIA-Initiated%20Remote%20Viewing%20At%20Stanford%20Research%20Institute.htm)). In January 2017 it put more than 12 million pages of its CREST database online, including the STAR GATE files ([Smithsonian 2017](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/over-12-million-pages-cia-documents-are-now-accessible-online-180961846/)).

## "On the possibility of directly accessing every human brain" (1995)

Persinger's 1995 paper in *Perceptual and Motor Skills* is a theoretical essay with no new experiment ([Persinger 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7567396/)).

## The God Helmet replication controversy (1997–2019)

Persinger's lab reported "sensed presences" from weak fields over the temporal lobes. In one study 9 of 15 volunteers exposed to 1 µT field bursts or sham fields reported one; the abstract does not separate the two conditions ([Cook & Persinger 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9347559/)). A double-blind study of 48 people found more presences with right-sided or bilateral fields than with left-sided or sham fields ([Persinger & Healey 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12193838/)). The lab described responses in "up to 80%" of subjects ([Granqvist 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15849873/)). All of this is **single-lab**.

| Tinoco & Ortiz 2014 | Unblinded partial replication, N = 20 | No difference during sessions ([Tinoco & Ortiz 2014](https://www.jcer.com/index.php/jcj/article/viewFile/361/386)) |
| Sham-helmet studies, 2014–2019 | Helmets that are never switched on | Expectation, absorption and belief produce the experiences ([van Elk 2014](https://dare.uva.nl/id/eb90400b-9a27-4892-9891-e2797972ccb4), [Maij & van Elk 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30355534/), [Maij et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/2153599x.2017.1403952), [Simmonds-Moore et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0276236617749185)) |

Persinger said of the Swedish study: "They didn't replicate it, not even close" ([Khamsi 2004](https://www.nature.com/news/2004/041206/full/news041206-10.html)). The dispute was never settled. In 2019 van Elk reported that the data of one unpublished student sham-helmet study "had been faked"; this does not concern the published studies above ([van Elk 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/10508619.2019.1687189)). Felt presences can be produced without weak fields: by electrical stimulation of the temporoparietal junction ([Arzy et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16988702/)) or by a robot that creates sensorimotor conflict ([Blanke et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25447995/)).

Verdict: a field-specific God Helmet effect is **single-lab**. That sham helmets produce such experiences is **established**.

Points that matter for a head-worn coil:
- **Epilepsy.** WHO notes that "People suffering from or predisposed to epilepsy are likely to be more susceptible to induced ELF electric fields in the central nervous system" ([EHC 238](https://inchem.org/documents/ehc/ehc/ehc238.pdf)).
- **Local sources.** ICNIRP: "For a very localized source with a distance of a few centimeters from the body, the only realistic option for the exposure assessment is to determine dosimetrically the induced electric field" ([ICNIRP 2010](https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPLFgdl.pdf)).
- **Implants.** Compliance may not prevent interference with pacemakers, defibrillators or cochlear implants ([ICNIRP 2010](https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPLFgdl.pdf)).
- **Research volunteers.** ICNIRP treats volunteers in medical research as "medical exposure", outside the general-public limits ([ICNIRP 2010](https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPLFgdl.pdf)). IEEE says its limits are not intended for "informed volunteers in scientific research studies" ([IEEE 2019](https://w.mriquestions.com/uploads/3/4/5/7/34572113/ieee_standards_08910342.pdf)).
- **Leukaemia data since 2002.** The link has weakened: a 2010 pooled analysis found an odds ratio of 1.44 (0.88–2.36) at ≥ 0.3 µT ([Kheifets et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20877339/)), and a 2022 one found 1.01 at ≥ 0.4 µT ([Amoon et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34481821/)). IARC has not re-evaluated.

How this helmet compares with these limits is worked out in [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md).

The helmet's field at the cortex is far below the 100 µT that the Navy gave men for many hours. Within about 1 cm of a coil face, at full current, it exceeds the Seafarer field, but only in that small volume and for at most an hour. [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) compares it with the ICNIRP limits. Because ICNIRP and IEEE leave research volunteers outside their limits, the general-public values are the sensible benchmark for self-experiment.

**What the record does and does not give us.**
- No document shows a working "mind-influence" device at microtesla ELF levels. The intelligence reports that speculated about weapons said they lacked data ([DIA 1976](https://archive.org/details/cia-and-ussr-research-on-electromagnetic-frequencies-collection), [NGIC 1998](https://archive.org/download/cia-and-ussr-research-on-electromagnetic-frequencies-collection/%5BArmy%5D%20Bioeffects%20of%20Selected%20Nonlethal%20Weapons.pdf)). This project makes no such claim, and nothing in the record supports one.
- In these records, the one documented coil exposure of a programme viewer is the 1998 Swann test. The **Programs** tab has it twice: `military-p06-persinger-swann-1998` (2 µT) and `persinger-swann-2002-table1` (3.5 µT, timing as printed in the paper). Both are **single-lab**.
- The programmes' lasting product is method: random targets from a fixed pool, blind rank-order judging against decoys, feedback only after judging ([AIR 1995](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4.pdf)). The **Experiment** and **Targets** tabs use that design, with hardware-random targets and a SHA-256 commitment; see [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md). Hyman's objections still apply to home trials: a single judge and reused targets weaken any result.
- The **Guides** tab has CRV, ARV, ERV and outbound-beacon sessions based on the declassified and published procedures, and Gateway-style sessions in our own wording ([GUIDES.md](GUIDES.md)). They copy structure, not proven effects; their evidence labels range from anecdote to single-lab.
- The optional Kp logging echoes the 1984 Geophysical Effects Study. The local-sidereal-time logging follows Spottiswoode's later analyses, whose 13.5 h peak a larger sample did not reproduce ([Psi Encyclopedia: James Spottiswoode](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/james-spottiswoode/)). Both sets of correlations were post hoc, so treat any split as exploratory ([EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md)).

**Blinding is the lesson of the replication record.** Independent double-blind tests found no field effect, and switched-off helmets produce the same experiences. Use the **Blind** option for any comparison. The preset `persinger-granqvist-2005-replication` (5 µT) recreates the Swedish test for blinded self-tests ([preset file](../presets/persinger-granqvist-2005-replication.json)). Record expectations and suggestibility with the questionnaires in [QUESTIONNAIRES.md](QUESTIONNAIRES.md).

**Belief can do harm.** The 1995 Persinger essay feeds "targeted individual" literature. Do not use this device, or this history, to persuade anyone that they are being influenced at a distance.
