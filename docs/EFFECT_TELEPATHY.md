# Telepathy: what is claimed, what laboratories found, and how to test it with two people

Telepathy is the claim that information passes from one person to another without any known channel. It is tied to the God Helmet because Michael Persinger's laboratory ran partner and sibling studies with its ring of eight coils, and because Todd Murphy, who sells the Shakti and Shiva devices, presents them in a lecture as proof that weak fields can switch telepathy on.

This article sets out how telepathy is tested, what 140 years of research found, what the Persinger papers report, where Murphy's account departs from them, and how to run a fair trial. The short version: ganzfeld meta-analyses still report a small effect, but it shrinks under stricter controls and no design produces it reliably. The God Helmet link rests on three small papers from one laboratory, none independently replicated.

## How to read this article

The evidence words are the ones used across the project:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **established** | Independently replicated |
| **single-lab** | Reported by one laboratory or research group only |
| **claim** | Stated by an author, agency or vendor without independent support |
| **anecdote** | A personal account |
| **unverified** | Could not be checked against a primary source |

Nothing about telepathy itself is **established** in this sense. The word applies here only to findings about method, such as the lower success rate of pre-registered studies.

## What telepathy is

Edmund Gurney, Frederic Myers and Frank Podmore of the Society for Psychical Research (SPR) defined it in 1886 as "the ability of one mind to impress or to be impressed by another mind otherwise than through the recognised channels of sense". They called the sender the *agent* and the receiver the *percipient* (*Phantasms of the Living*, 1886, vol. 1, p. 6). Four kinds of evidence are offered:

| Design | How it works | How it is scored |
|---|---|---|
| **Spontaneous cases** | People report an impression or apparition of someone at the moment of that person's death or danger | Coincidences against an estimate of chance |
| **Forced choice** | A receiver guesses which of a few known symbols is the target | Hits against chance, 20 % with five symbols |
| **Free response** | A receiver who is dreaming, or in the ganzfeld (eyes covered, steady noise), describes impressions while a distant sender looks at a picture | The receiver or a judge ranks the target among decoys; with four pictures chance is 25 % |
| **Physiology** | One person is stimulated; a distant partner's EEG or skin response is recorded | Stimulation periods against control periods |

**A sender does not make a result telepathy.** A hit could also come from the receiver perceiving the picture itself, if such an ability exists. Comparisons mostly find that the sender makes no difference: 34 % hits with a known sender and with none in an Edinburgh series, and no noticeable difference at SAIC ([Utts 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/air)). One meta-analysis found about twice the effect in sender designs (.08) as in designs without one (.04), with overlapping intervals ([Tressoldi & Storm 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11134153/)). Researchers therefore speak of "anomalous information transfer".

## The history in brief

### Crisis apparitions (1886–1894)

*Phantasms of the Living* collected accounts of apparitions and impressions that coincided with a distant person's death or crisis, and argued that telepathy explained them (**anecdote**; the explanation is a **claim**). The SPR followed with a census. Of 17,000 people asked whether they had ever had a waking hallucination, 9.9 % said yes. The committee counted 30 apparitions seen within 12 hours of the person's death among about 1,300 recognised apparitions: 1 in 43, where the death rate predicted 1 in 19,000, or "440 times" chance (Sidgwick's committee, *Proceedings of the SPR* 10, 1894, pp. 36–39, 245–247) (**claim**).

The SPR's own Donald West later re-examined the census. Old cases were over-represented (51 against 29 expected), which suggests that memories gain coincidences with time. Of the 26 printed death cases, only six claimed a note written before the news arrived, and one note survived. He called the result "inconclusive" (West, *Proceedings of the SPR* 48, pp. 281, 288–289).

### Card guessing at Duke (1930s)

In J. B. Rhine's Pearce–Pratt series of 1933–34, with subject and cards in different buildings at least 100 yards apart, Hubert Pearce scored 558 hits in 1,850 guesses where 370 were expected (Rhine & Pratt 1957, pp. 46–47; Mishlove, *The Roots of Consciousness*, rev. ed., p. 285). It was a clairvoyance test: nobody looked at the cards. The critic C. E. M. Hansel showed that Pearce had been left unobserved. Rhine and Pratt also counted S. G. Soal's London experiments as conclusive; Soal's data were later found to have been manipulated (Mishlove, p. 285). The Duke results are **single-lab**. Mishlove, a proponent, notes that such early studies are rarely cited as evidence today (p. 284).

### Dream telepathy at Maimonides (1964–1978)

At the Maimonides Medical Center in Brooklyn, Montague Ullman and Stanley Krippner woke sleepers after each dream period, while a sender in another room concentrated on an art print. Judges ranked the prints against the dream reports in 13 formal and 3 pilot studies ([Psi Encyclopedia](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/maimonides-dream-telepathy-research/)).

A meta-analysis of 50 dream studies from 1966 to 2016 gives an effect size of .33 for the 14 Maimonides studies and .14 for the 36 others (not a significant difference), and a decline over the years. Telepathy, clairvoyance and precognition designs did not differ ([Storm et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.11588/ijodr.2017.2.34888)). Two replication attempts by Foulkes and colleagues failed, the second with Krippner, Honorton and Ullman among its authors ([Belvedere & Foulkes 1971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4331356/); [Foulkes et al. 1972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4345383/)). The Maimonides effect is **single-lab**; the later record is mixed.

### The ganzfeld (1974 to today)

The ganzfeld replaced sleep with mild sensory reduction.

| Analysis | Result | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 28 studies to 1985 (Honorton, in [Bem & Honorton 1994](https://rainbow.ldeo.columbia.edu/~alexeyk/Papers/BemHonorton1994.pdf)) | 23 positive, combined z = 6.60, about 35 % hits against 25 % | The sceptic Ray Hyman and Honorton agreed in a 1986 joint communiqué on "an overall significant effect" not explained by "selective reporting or multiple analysis", with the verdict to await experiments under "more stringent standards" |
| Autoganzfeld 1983–89 ([Bem & Honorton 1994](https://rainbow.ldeo.columbia.edu/~alexeyk/Papers/BemHonorton1994.pdf)) | 106 hits in 329 sessions, 32 % | Sound from the sender's room may have reached the experimenter ([Wiseman, Smith & Kornbrot](https://metabunk.org/attachments/auditory-leakage-autoganzfeld-pdf.60688)) |
| [Milton & Wiseman 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10414223/) | 30 studies from 7 laboratories: effect size 0.013, not significant | "does not at present offer a replicable method" |
| [Storm, Tressoldi & Di Risio 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20565164/) | 29 studies, 1997–2008: effect size .142 | The Bayes factor falls from about 6 billion to at most 330 without manually randomised studies and with omitted studies added ([Rouder, Morey & Province 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23294092/)) |
| [Tressoldi & Storm 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11134153/), registered report | 78 studies: effect size about .08, 6.8 points above chance | Median study power .088; selected participants .13, unselected .04 |
| [Watt, KPU 1074](https://www.koestler-parapsychology.psy.ed.ac.uk/Documents/KPU_1074_ResultsSummary.pdf), pre-registered | 72 hits in 240 sessions, 30 %, one-tailed p = .043 | A precognition design with no sender |

Honorton's first database suggested that friends make better senders (44 % against 26 %). His autoganzfeld did not confirm it (35 % against 29 %, not significant) ([Bem & Honorton 1994](https://rainbow.ldeo.columbia.edu/~alexeyk/Papers/BemHonorton1994.pdf)). The autoganzfeld result is **single-lab**. The pooled effect is a **claim** of proponent meta-analyses that critics dispute.

### Twins, siblings and EEG

A 1965 note in *Science* reported that evoking alpha rhythm in one identical twin elicited it in the other ([Duane & Behrendt 1965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5890891/)). A 2003 study in two shielded rooms found EEG changes in unstimulated partners while the other person watched a pattern, "although no biophysical mechanism is known" ([Wackermann et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12493602/)). Both are **single-lab**. [Linked brains](EFFECT_LINKED_BRAINS.md) covers this line in depth.

## The God Helmet record

### The three papers

All three used the lab's ring of eight solenoids around the head (the "Octopus"), with the field stepping from coil to coil ([The ring](EFFECT_RING.md); [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)).

| Study | Protocol | Reported outcome | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Persinger, Cook & Tiller 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12434847/) | One person in the ring gave a free narrative. An "emotionally related" partner at the same time recalled shared memories about a randomly chosen stimulus. 44 students matched the texts blind | Matching significant for 5 of the 7 pairs whose narratives were long enough. Congruence fell with geomagnetic activity (rho = −.72) | **single-lab** |
| [Booth, Charette & Persinger 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12434850/) | 15 couples. The woman was exposed; the man wrote memories evoked by one of five postcards | The abstract gives no hit rate. Ranking was *more* accurate with higher geomagnetic activity, the opposite sign | **single-lab** |
| [Persinger, Koren & Tsang 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14738355/) | 4 sibling pairs. One sibling in the ring, about 1 µT; the other's EEG recorded in another room | More 5–6 Hz power over the frontal and occipital lobes during one of the six patterns | **single-lab** |

The later studies with strangers, paired rings and light flashes are in [Linked brains](EFFECT_LINKED_BRAINS.md). The 2003 paper's title asks whether its result is "a mechanism for cognitive influence at a distance"; [Influence at a distance](EFFECT_REMOTE_INFLUENCE.md) takes that up.

### What Murphy says, and what the papers say

Murphy's lecture is a vendor's account (**claim**). We compared it with the abstracts and with the lab's 2010 retelling ([Persinger & Lavallee 2010](https://jcer.com/index.php/jcj/article/view/99)). The papers' full texts are closed and were not read.

| Murphy | The record |
|---|---|
| Pairs were "brothers-sisters, mothers and daughters, twins, and married couples" ([12:20](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=740)) | The abstract says only "emotionally related individuals". The companion study used 15 couples |
| 44 students rated the matches, which "came up 2.85 times more than they would have by chance alone" ([13:53](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=833)) | 44 students is right. The abstract gives no ratio, only "5 of the 7 pairs". The 2.85 figure is **unverified** |
| With the "chirp" signal "their accuracy improved" ([14:34](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=874)) | The abstracts describe no sessions without a field to improve on. No Laurentian paper defines a chirp ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)) |
| When one sibling pretended to touch the other, the other's EEG responded and the times matched "perfectly" ([15:20](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=920), [16:24](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=984)) | The abstract reports a power rise during one pattern and no touching task. The 2010 retelling adds the imagined touch, but names a different pattern and brain region, and says EEG was sampled for 20 s per 5-minute pattern |
| "No one has published anything criticizing any of these experiments" ([16:37](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA?t=997)) | We found no independent replication either. Silence is not confirmation |

Murphy's Shakti design for psychic skills carries his note that "none of the procedures described on this page have been tested yet" ([Murphy: psychic skills](https://www.god-helmet.com/wp/winshakti/demo/shakti_demo/psychic.htm)) (**unverified**).

### What is missing

- **A sham arm.** The abstracts compare patterns with each other, not field with no field. The 2010 retelling says the sibling effect was absent without the field, and gives no data.
- **Numbers.** Four and seven pairs. One pattern of six stood out; the abstract mentions no correction for testing six.
- **Consistency.** Two papers report opposite geomagnetic correlations ([Geomagnetic activity](EFFECT_GEOMAGNETIC.md)).
- **Replication and pre-registration.** None.

Independent double-blind tests of God-Helmet-type fields on experience found no field effect ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)). We found no test outside Persinger's lab of whether such fields change telepathy scores.

## The debate

| Issue | What is known |
|---|---|
| **Effect size** | About 31 % hits against 25 % in 78 studies ([Bierman, Spottiswoode & Bijl 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153049)). Most single studies are too small to detect that ([Tressoldi & Storm 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11134153/)) |
| **File drawer** | Honorton estimated that 423 unreported null studies would be needed to cancel the first database ([Bem & Honorton 1994](https://rainbow.ldeo.columbia.edu/~alexeyk/Papers/BemHonorton1994.pdf)). Critics answer that "there are almost certainly omitted replication failures" ([Rouder, Morey & Province 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23294092/)) |
| **Sensory leakage** | Sounds from the sender, handled target pictures. Possible in the autoganzfeld, not demonstrated ([Wiseman, Smith & Kornbrot](https://metabunk.org/attachments/auditory-leakage-autoganzfeld-pdf.60688)) |
| **Optional stopping, flexible analysis, manual randomisation** | Modelled questionable practices could explain about 60 % of the ganzfeld effect; the rest stayed significant, p = .003 (Bierman et al. 2016). Manually randomised studies show larger effects (Rouder et al. 2013). Judging: [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) |
| **Pre-registration** | 16 % of results in studies pre-registered with the Koestler Unit were significant, against 46.6 % in unregistered studies ([Wiseman & Watt 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42222493/)) (**established**: an earlier registered-report scheme showed the same pattern, [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md)) |

Reviewers read the record in opposite ways. One holds that the evidence is "comparable to that for established phenomena in psychology" ([Cardeña 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29792448/)). A reply holds that the claims "cannot be true" given well-understood scientific principles, so positive data must be flawed ([Reber & Alcock 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31192620/)).

**What would change minds.** The model is the Transparent Psi Project: a design agreed in advance by proponents and sceptics, run in many laboratories with audited data. For a precognition task it found 49.89 % against 50 % chance ([Kekecs et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36756055/)). We found no such test of telepathy with a sender. A clear positive would oblige sceptics to take the effect seriously; a null would leave proponents with meta-analyses of small studies.

### A fair test

[EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) has the full method. The essentials:

1. **Two people who both know and agree.** Never "send" to someone who has not consented.
2. **Pre-register** the hypothesis, one primary outcome, the number of trials and who judges.
3. **Have a third person build the picture pool** in [Trials → Picture pool](#trials/pictures). Both phones import it unseen.
4. **Create the trial** in [Trials](#trials): mode "Telepathy (with a sender)", a program, **Blind** ticked. The helmet's random generator picks the target and real or sham, and commits to them (SHA-256) before the receiver answers.
5. **Separate.** The sender goes to a room that is not next door, opens **Trials → Sender** on a second phone and looks at the target in silence. No calls or messages before the reveal.
6. **Respond, lock, judge blind, reveal.** The receiver writes, locks the response and ranks the pictures; an outside judge is better.
7. **Analyse once**, at the planned number of trials. Aborted trials stay counted.

### How many trials

With four pictures and a true hit rate of 32 % (the autoganzfeld level) against 25 % chance, 279 trials give 80 % power at one-sided α = .05; a true rate of 30 % needs 522 ([EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md)). Showing that the helmet adds anything needs more still. A series of 20 or 30 trials is a pilot.

### What it cannot show

- **A hit rate above chance says nothing about the helmet.** Only real better than sham does.
- **It cannot separate telepathy from other routes.** That needs trials in which, by a hidden coin toss, nobody views the target. The app does not automate this.
- **A home series cannot rule out leakage** as a laboratory can. Expect chance, and report every trial, including the nulls.

The helmet is not a treatment for anything; [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) applies to every session.
