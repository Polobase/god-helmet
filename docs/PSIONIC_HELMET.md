# The Psionic Helmet and the Disclosure Day device: what they share with the God Helmet

"Psionic helmet" is the name hobbyists give to headgear that is said to amplify psychic ability. One example is the "Daydreamer Psionic Helmet", shown by its maker in a 25-minute video. In Steven Spielberg's film *Disclosure Day* (2026), a character lies wired with forehead electrodes while an alien object lets him enter another person's mind. Both invite comparison with the God Helmet, the coil helmet of Michael Persinger and Stanley Koren that this project rebuilds.

This article sets out what each device is, what is claimed for it, what evidence exists and what a fair test would be. The short version: they share a look and a setting, not a mechanism. The Daydreamer is a hard hat with dials, flashing lights, a chant player and a small pulsed coil block; we found no test of it or of any helmet like it. It comes from the radionics hobby. The UFO disclosure movement uses "psionic" for people and, in the sources we read, shows no helmet. The film's device is fiction. What all of them share with the God Helmet and the ganzfeld is the part that works without any field: a helmet, quiet, closed or covered eyes, and expectation.

## How to read this article

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **established** | Independently replicated |
| **single-lab** | Reported by one laboratory or research group only |
| **claim** | Stated by an author, agency or vendor without independent support |
| **anecdote** | A personal account |
| **unverified** | Could not be checked against a primary source |

Nothing about a psionic helmet is **established**. The word applies here to two findings only: flashing light can trigger seizures in photosensitive people, and inert helmets produce unusual experiences in people who expect them. A film is not evidence of any kind. Makers are quoted from their own public videos and books. Nothing here is guidance for observing or influencing anyone.

## One word, three uses

"Psionics" joins psi and electronics: machines that are supposed to work with psychic ability.

| When | Who | What "psionic" means | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1950s | The science-fiction editor John W. Campbell; the inventor T. Galen Hieronymus | A patented "analyser", read by stroking a pad until the finger sticks | **claim** |
| From 1977 | Charles Cosimano and small sellers | A dial box and a helmet that "tune" the wearer to a distant person | **claim** |
| By 2019 | Guests of Gaia's *Cosmic Disclosure*; later the Skywatcher group | Psychic ability itself; "psionic assets" said to call craft | **claim** |

- **The word.** The *Encyclopedia of Science Fiction* finds it first in a Jack Williamson story in *Astounding* (July 1951) and calls Campbell an "eager advocate of psionics" ([SFE](https://www.sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/psionics)). The *Encyclopedia of Occultism & Parapsychology* says Campbell coined it (5th ed., p. 1246).
- **The machine.** Hieronymus's patent claims a detector whose surface feels different to the operator's fingers ([US 2,482,773](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2482773A/en), granted 1949). Campbell reported that a drawing of the circuit worked as well as the machine (Tompkins & Bird 1973, p. 350; Cosimano, *Psionic Power*, p. 59) (**claim**). If so, the electronics are a symbol and the working part is the operator.
- **A test.** The same encyclopedia calls a 1950 test of a radionic practitioner under American Medical Association auspices "completely negative" (p. 1273). We did not read the original report (**unverified**).

## The Daydreamer Psionic Helmet

**Who presents it.** The YouTube channel "Jobet Claudio" carries the tutorial, viewed about 640 times. The presenter says he is an engineer from "Metamind Technologies" and calls the helmet one of its fastest-selling products ([0:58](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=58)). He names no price. We found no sales page, company record or review, so we cannot say who else stands behind it.

**What the video shows.** A yellow hard hat. On the front are three numbered dials and a clip for a "witness", a photograph of the target person. At the back are a power switch, a 5 V socket and a jack for a radionic box, and on top a folding aerial. By his account the crown is filled with "orgonite" resin holding a "proprietary configuration of coils", driven by a battery and a five-mode pulser ([7:52](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=472), [20:39](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=1239)). Red LEDs under the brim flash onto the closed eyelids ([8:38](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=518)). An MP3 player plays mantras and "spells from Filipino folk magic" ([11:02](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=662)). He calls it a combination of Cosimano's three-dial box and "psionic amplifier helmet" ([1:40](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=100)).

**How it is used.** The wearer rubs a "stick pad" while turning each dial until the finger seems to stick ([14:32](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=872)), then puts the helmet on and relaxes with closed eyes.

**What is claimed** (all **claim**):
- it "can improve anything that has to do with psychic abilities": altered states, remote viewing, "manifesting", reading minds, seeing through another person's eyes ([4:02](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=242));
- thoughts can be "broadcast" to the person in the photograph ([18:43](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=1123));
- the flashing light gives "photonic brainwave entrainment", brain rhythms following the light into deeper states ([8:52](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=532));
- with a sigil in the clip, easier contact with angels, demons or jinn ([22:30](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=1350)).

He adds: "your results may vary, I will not guarantee that you'll be able to do this" ([18:20](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=1100)).

**The evidence offered** is one story (**anecdote**). In 1996 or 1997 he built Cosimano's helmet, tuned it to a room-mate's photograph and "saw" him at night in a car with three other people. The room-mate objected that it had been a pickup. The presenter took that as "perfect confirmation", since from inside he could not have told ([5:32](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=332) to [7:21](https://youtu.be/667_v4qrrz0?t=441)). He mentions no written record, random target or independent judge.

**What is not stated:** field strength, pulse rates, flash rates, or any warning about flashing light.

**Where it comes from.** Cosimano's own instructions describe a passive hard hat: three potentiometers ("the value does not matter"), eight pieces of magnet strip, a foil disc and a wire coil at the crown, and a jack (*Elementary Psionics*, pp. 88–92). He says the magnets make a weak field that raises the brain's psychic output (p. 96) (**claim**). He also says the idea "was not original with me": psychic amplifier helmets were "a staple of science fiction writers and comic books" (p. 88). In a video he says his first talisman hat "sort of worked" and may have been "psychosomatic" ([5:24](https://youtu.be/Fsghn8lda4Q?t=324)). The twelve Cosimano texts we searched never mention Persinger. Others sell variants: a 1993 advertisement lists a "Cosimano PSI Helmet" (*Mezlim* 4(4), p. 10), and a recent video offers a "Tepaphone Psionic Helmet" for $175 ([video](https://youtu.be/JdnifET3X3A)).

## "Psionics" in the disclosure movement

- **Gaia.** In *Cosmic Disclosure*, the guest Randy Cramer describes "psionic amplifiers", some worn as a "halo" on the head or neck, "amplifying everything out of the brain" ([season 20, episode 3](https://www.gaia.com/video/psionic-warfare-et-agendas)) (**claim**; a personal account with no support).
- **Jake Barber.** In January 2025 the former US Air Force airman told NewsNation of "psionics" who help to summon craft. *The Debrief* asked whether the claims are "simply too outrageous to be true" ([Hanks 2025](https://thedebrief.org/covert-controversy-the-jacob-barber-uap-allegations/)). His group Skywatcher says it tried this in August 2024 ([Liberation Times 2025](https://liberationtimes.squarespace.com/home/can-ufos-be-summoned-or-signalled-a-new-scientific-initiative-has-plans-to-verify-such-claims-this-year)) (**claim**).
- **Skywatcher's own caveats.** "We are not yet at the stage of independent verification." The data on the mental method are "still sparse and not yet rigorously vetted" (same article). In November 2026 the group's research page listed no independently verified result and called its hypotheses "exploratory" ([Skywatcher](https://www.skywatcher.ai/research)).
- **Sceptics and scholars.** Forum analysts noted that the video shown with the story has "no chain of custody" ([Metabunk 2025](https://www.metabunk.org/goto/post?id=333736)). A religious-studies researcher writes that such operations "are not new or unique but reflect motifs that have long been present" in contact stories ([Dunn 2025](https://religion.osu.edu/events/spirituality-and-technoscience-ufo-contact-modalities)).
- **No helmet.** None of these sources shows a psionic helmet. Skywatcher plans to record "brainwave activity (EEGs) and heart rate variability"; EEG sensors record and do not stimulate.
- **The film as disclosure.** In [Disclosure No Longer Top Secret](https://youtu.be/guaXM5Ghan0?t=140) (*Awakening Cosmic Reality Show*), the presenter describes the film's objects as hand-held "crystalline rods" that connect to other minds, says "this technology is real" and calls the film "a kind of psychological operation" (**claim**; the captions look machine-translated). The video mentions no helmet.

## The device in *Disclosure Day*

*Disclosure Day* was directed by Steven Spielberg from a screenplay by David Koepp and released by Universal on 12 June 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disclosure_Day)). *Mild spoilers for the set-up follow.*

- **In the trailer.** Colin Firth's character holds a small glowing object beside a screen ([1:24](https://youtu.be/SCYT8vb2siQ?t=84)). Later he lies back in a chair with white electrode pads wired to his forehead and temple, and his eyes darken and glow ([1:42](https://youtu.be/SCYT8vb2siQ?t=102), [2:15](https://youtu.be/SCYT8vb2siQ?t=135)).
- **In reviews.** The object is "The Device", alien technology. He "uses his own Device to infiltrate" another character's mind ([SlashFilm 2026](https://slashfilm.com/2192691/disclosure-day-aliens-revealed-ending-explained/)).
- **The thumbnail.** The widely seen [Pitch Meeting](https://youtu.be/AEOTI6959nM) thumbnail paints black discs over three actors' eyes. In the three official trailers, sampled every two seconds, we saw no eye covers, only eyes that change colour.
- **What it resembles.** Pads like those of an EEG or a hospital monitor, and a reclining chair. A journalist who sat in Persinger's chamber in 1999 describes a recliner, EEG electrodes on his scalp and halved ping-pong balls taped over his eyes ([Hitt 1999](https://web.archive.org/web/20201219182657/https://www.wired.com/1999/11/persinger/)). In the story the electrodes are not the working part; the alien object is.

A film prop is not evidence. If anything the traffic runs the other way: Cosimano took the helmet idea from science fiction.

## What they share

- **A helmet that announces an effect.** 193 festival visitors wore a skate helmet wired to a dummy box, blindfolded and in white noise, for 15 minutes. They reported out-of-body feelings, involuntary movements and felt presences; self-rated spirituality predicted who did ([Maij, van Elk & Schjoedt 2019](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/34902680/The_role_of_alcohol_in_expectancy_driven_mystical_experiences.pdf)). In the double-blind test of God Helmet fields (N = 89), suggestibility traits predicted the experiences and the field did not ([Granqvist et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15849873/)). Sham-helmet effects are **established** ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md) lists the studies).
- **Quiet, rest and covered eyes.** These alone alter experience: in 19 people, ganzfeld stimulation produced an altered state and loosened the coupling between thalamus and cortex ([Schmidt et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33122651/)).
- **Weak magnetism at the head.** Cosimano's magnets, the Daydreamer's coils, the God Helmet's patterns. Only the last has a stated dose, and no independent test has found it to act.

## Where they differ

- **The target.** The God Helmet and the ganzfeld act on the person in them. The psionic helmet and the film device are aimed at somebody else. This helmet's field falls to household background about ten centimetres from a coil ([Influence at a distance](EFFECT_REMOTE_INFLUENCE.md)).
- **The detector.** The "stick" is felt by an operator who knows the question. The likeliest ordinary explanation is the pendulum's: small unconscious movements (our reading, **unverified** for the stick pad).
- **Flicker is not ganzfeld.** The ganzfeld uses a steady field. Flicker produces more simple hallucinations ([Shenyan et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38287084/)), and it carries a seizure risk.
- **Sound.** Gateway-style audio uses binaural beats, whose effect on brain rhythms is inconsistent ([HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)). The Daydreamer plays chants.

## What has been tested

We searched PubMed and Europe PMC in November 2026 for "psionic helmet", "psionic amplifier", "psionic" and "orgonite", and the web and YouTube for the Daydreamer. We found no trial, measurement or independent review of any psionic helmet. The nearest tested device is a hand-held "molecular detector" sold to security forces. In a double-blind test it found a target hidden in one of eight boxes 3 times in 20, as chance predicts ([Mochán & Ramírez-Solís 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3971)) (**single-lab**).

## Safety

- **Flashing light.** An abnormal response to flashing light occurs in about 0.3 to 3 % of people. Flashes of 15 to 25 Hz provoke most, the range is 1 to 65 Hz, and red adds to the risk. In a television incident in Japan, only 24 % of the children who had a seizure had had one before ([Fisher et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16146439/)) (**established**). A red light flashing on the eyelids at an unstated rate is therefore not harmless by default.
- **Unknown coils and magnets.** No field is stated, so the exposure cannot be judged.
- **This project.** The app never uses flicker ([GUIDES.md](GUIDES.md)). [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) forbids light or sound machines and magnets during a session. Never wear or run two devices together.

### A fair test

[EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) has the method. Three questions, kept apart:

1. **Does the helmet matter?** Tick **Blind**: the helmet's random generator picks real or sham and reveals it after you have rated the session. Another device would need the same: a helper sets it on or off by coin toss, unseen by the wearer, with the lights off in both arms. That is a design, not advice to wear one.
2. **Does the tuning find anything?** A target hidden in one of eight identical containers; the real device against a look-alike copy. Chance is 1 in 8, and 6 or more hits in 20 happen by chance about 3 % of the time.
3. **Is anything received?** A target committed by SHA-256 before the response, a consenting partner, a judge who does not know the target. With four pictures, 279 trials are needed to tell 32 % from 25 %.

Summoning craft is beyond this project. A test would need session times drawn at random, sensors run by people who do not know whether a session is on, and all data published.

### What it cannot show

- **A striking session shows nothing.** Inert helmets produce them. Only real better than sham counts.
- **This helmet cannot test another device,** and a film cannot be tested at all.
- **Consent comes first.** Every trial needs the informed agreement of everyone in it. "Sending" to a person who does not know is not an experiment this project supports.

The helmet is not a treatment for anything; [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) applies to every session.
