# Library notes: the 8 Coil Shakti

These are reading notes. They were taken in November 2026 while every program of the library was checked against its sources ([PRESET_AUDIT.md](PRESET_AUDIT.md)), and they are published as they were taken: one row per statement, with the page and, where it is short, the passage. Pages are PDF page numbers with the printed page in brackets, for example `p.3 [661]`. The last column says how a statement was read: `image` on the page image, `text` in the text layer only (scans confuse µT with mT and drop minus signs, so no decision rests on such a value alone), `web` on a web page. Statements about the vendor's products come from the vendor's own pages and are claims. Nothing here was measured on a helmet.

Abbreviations: `SD/` = https://www.god-helmet.com/wp/winshakti/demo/shakti_demo/ · `ROT/` = https://www.god-helmet.com/wp/winshakti/rotating/ · `GH/` = https://www.god-helmet.com/wp/god-helmet/ · `SH/` = https://www.god-helmet.com/wp/shiva/shiva_demo/ · `PS/` = an Internet Archive copy of the vendor's Paranormal Shakti demo. ## 8 Coil Shakti as described

**Coil count and wiring.** Eight coils, four per stereo channel, each channel made of two pairs, each pair one + and one − coil. The vendor, SD/getting_started_basic.htm, 'NOTE FOR 8-COIL PLACEMENT': "There are four types of Coils. Positive, and Negative. Left and right. One pair of coils has one positive (+) and one negative (-). Each side of the head gets two pairs. One pair is from the left stereo channel, and the other pair is from the right stereo channel." And SD/8_coil_placement.htm: "You need TWO poles (Positive and Negative) to create ONE magnetic field." The coil type is not named anywhere I read ("modified commonly available magnetic coils", SD/mono_proposal.htm); Gendle calls them "solenoids" (p.4 [220]). Turns, size, resistance and inductance: not stated in any source of this family.

**Stereo channels and sides.** The L and R marks are channels, not sides: "The left and right markings on the coils do NOT refer to left and right sides of your head. They refer to left and right stereo channels. Putting all the L coils on the left and R coils on the right is incorrect setup." (SD/getting_started_basic.htm). The chart on that page (an HTML table, read from the markup) gives the standard set-up:

| side of head | top | front | back | bottom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| left | L − | R − | R + | L + |
| right | L + | R + | R − | L − |

**Placement in the vendor's words.** No 10-20 names and no distances from landmarks anywhere. "Make sure that the coils from one of your stereo channels are at the top and bottom of a cross, and the other stereo channel's coils are at the front and back." · "Using a baseball cap to apply the signals to the temporal lobes" · "This is the placement used in the Koren Helmet" (SD/rot_temporal.htm). "One pair is vertical, and the other is horizontal." · "This is the arrangement that is closest to the original laboratory setup for the 'God Helmet'." (SD/8_coil_placement.htm). For the modulated 40 Hz signal: "put the coils about an inch above each ear" (SD/obe45_consent.htm). "The coil placement does not have to be exact." (PS/Paranormal_hipp_consent.htm). The cross at FT7, C5, TP7, T9 / FT8, C6, TP8, T10 is therefore the project's reading of "a cross over the temporal lobe": the shape is the vendor's, the sites are not.

**Alternation.** "The first version of the software for the 8-Coil unit switched the signals from one channel to the other slowly - one 'switch' after one instance of the signal. This version includes "fast' alternation, in which the signals alternate between channels twice each second. ... In lab tests, it emerged as the most effective." (SD/rot_principles.htm, 'FAST AND SLOW ALTERNATION'). Not every session alternates: "Not all session designs for the 8-coil Shakti use alternating signals" (SD/feelgood_consent.htm).

**Session rules.** "Right now, only three schedules are supported ... Every 72 hours / Once a week / Once a month" · "DO NOT DO SESSIONS EVERY DAY." · "It is NOT suggested that you go back and forth between two session designs" (SD/how_often_is_safe.htm). "NEVER do the same session two days in a row. There are only four durations that are tested ... 10 minutes ... test sessions ... 20 minutes - the shortest sessions done in published studies. 30 minutes - These are primarily intended for series of sessions ... 60 minutes - These are not intended to be done more often than once every three weeks." (SD/safe_durations.htm). "Do not do more than one hour-long session in three weeks (Excepting two-phase sessions)." and blindfold, silence, earplugs for hour-long sessions (SD/hour.htm). Series: three or six sessions, then three weeks off; three months off after a year (SD/break.htm, SD/threedays.htm, SD/weekly.htm). One-sided signals need a 10-min test on each side first, starting with the unpleasant side (SD/testsession.htm). No meditation or other devices during a session; other devices about two hours later, three to five per day at most (ROT/rot_safety.htm). Volume never above 92 % (SD/duds.htm).

Reading of the table (mine, not a source statement): the vendor's numbers span a factor of nine and never say where. Gendle's instrument is a hand-held meter giving RMS values; the same paper reports 0.1 µT room background and 0.1–0.2 µT from the coils with the program off, which is the lower end of the 0.1–1.0 µT reading, and its frequency response to 3-ms-point bursts is not given (the maker's page could not be fetched). A coil small enough to sit on a cap cannot give 1 µT at its face and still 0.1–1 µT at 9 cm, so either the vendor's 10 mG is not a reading at the coil or the centre reading is mostly background. For this helmet, `uT: 1` means 1 µT at 20 mm, about 24 µT at the scalp and 56 µT at the coil face, and about 0.02 µT per coil at 9 cm (r⁻³ extrapolation of this helmet model; at most about 0.15 µT at full current). The comparison with either source figure is therefore open by more than a factor of ten in both directions.

**Which signals are defined by numbers.** None of the vendor's waveforms is published as point values. What the vendor does state in numbers: 3-ms points for the standard signals and 1-ms points for the "high-speed" ones; about 4 s of silence; about 9.6 s of field per minute; a 5.4 Hz spectral peak for the LTP signal; fast alternation twice per second; a 16-bit PCM source file ("44109hz" [sic], SD/mono_proposal.htm); "most of the signals are about 260 milliseconds" (SD/duds.htm, in the text on the volume plug-ins; this does not fit 230 points of 3 ms = 690 ms, nor 9.6 s per minute with 4-s gaps). The only signals in this family with published numbers come from the lab papers, and then only as point count, point duration and repeat interval: burst-firing 230 points × 3 ms once every 4 s (Ryczko 2002 p.1 [592], citing Fleming 1994) or × 4 ms once every 3 s (Ryczko's own exposure, p.2 [593]); Thomas pattern 837 points × 1 ms, 4 s apart (Dixon 2001 p.4 [90]). The point values themselves are in none of these sources. Not defined by any number: caudate, Fibonacci, modulated 40 Hz, the modulated 40 Hz chirp, the "slow" hippocampal signal, the short-latency amygdala signal.

## Places on the head that a source states

No source of this family gives a 10-20 or 10-10 site, an MNI or Talairach coordinate, or a measured distance from a landmark for a coil. What they give in words:

- Temporal cross: one channel's coils "at the top and bottom of a cross", the other's "at the front and back" (SD/rot_temporal.htm); worn on a baseball cap.
- "about an inch above each ear" for a two-coil bilateral temporal placement (SD/obe45_consent.htm).
- "one coil in front of and below the right ear, and one coil over the angular gyrus on the right side" (SD/obe45_consent.htm).
- "one or two coils over each eye" (SH/mono_sdu.htm); frontal Feelgood coils shown in photos only.
- "the coils were placed about two inches forward of their usual locations" for the TV episode; cross "raised up, so they are above the temporo-parietal region" (PS/on-site_sessions.htm, PS/Paranormal_hipp_consent.htm).
- OBE area: "temporal lobes, parietal lobes or the area behind the parietal lobes – if you were a cow, it would be the place where your horns were" (ROT/shakti_obe.htm).
- Gendle 2012: "over the temporal lobes as per manufacturer guidelines" (p.4 [220]); gaussmeter "in the center of the headpiece (approximately 9 cm from the device's solenoids)" (p.11 [227]).
- Richards 1992: "four pairs of solenoids on each side along the plane of the temporal lobes" in a motorcycle helmet (p.2 [668]).
- Ross 2008: "left prefrontal region just over the eyebrow" (p.2, text layer).
