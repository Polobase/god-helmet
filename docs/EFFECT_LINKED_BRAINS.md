# Linked brains: "excess correlation" between two people in the same rotating field

The claim is that two people at a distance, each wearing a ring of coils that plays the same rotating field, become linked: a light flashed at one shows up in the EEG of the other. Michael Persinger's laboratory at Laurentian University reported effects of this kind from 2003 to 2015, later with cell cultures, chemical reactions and beakers of water in place of people. It called the effect "excess correlation" and "macroscopic entanglement". The ring is the lab's "Octopus", the eight-coil device this helmet can imitate ([The ring](EFFECT_RING.md)).

This article sets out the record, the physics and the older EEG studies the authors lean on, the ordinary explanations, and what a fair test would need. The short version: every result comes from one laboratory, with two to five pairs of people where a number is given, most of it in journals outside the mainstream indexes, with no preregistration and no independent replication. By standard physics, entanglement cannot carry a signal from one person to another. The older "transferred potential" literature failed its most careful replication. This project cannot test the EEG claim; the telepathy trial in the app is the honest stand-in.

## How to read this article

The evidence words are the ones used across the project:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **established** | Independently replicated |
| **single-lab** | Reported by one laboratory or research group only |
| **claim** | Stated by an author, agency or vendor without independent support |
| **anecdote** | A personal account |
| **unverified** | Could not be checked against a primary source |

Nothing claimed here is **established**. The word applies only to the physics that counts against it: entanglement is real, and it cannot be used to send a signal.

Nothing here is a method for influencing anyone. Every design below needs two volunteers who know what is being tested and agree to it.

## What is claimed

| Design | Who is in a field | Stimulus | What is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| One ring (2002–2008) | Only the "stimulus person" | The field, while that person imagines being with the partner | The partner's EEG or written impressions, in another room |
| Two rings, "yoked" (2010–2015) | Both, with identical fields | Light flashes, tones or a chemical injection at one place | EEG, light emission, pH or cell counts at the other |

- **The name.** The lab describes entanglement as "excess correlation between separated parts of a quantum system" and argues that identical fields make two places "become the same space" ([Persinger & Lavallee 2010](https://jcer.com/index.php/jcj/article/view/99)).
- **The recipe.** From 2012 the effect is said to need two steps: a rotation that speeds up (the "primer", "20+2"), then one that slows down (the "effector", "20−2"), after which it lasts about eight minutes ([Dotta & Persinger 2012](https://file.scirp.org/Html/17181.html); notation in [The ring](EFFECT_RING.md)).
- **The score.** A difference at the distant place between stimulus-on and stimulus-off periods, with the field and not without it.

### Where it was published

- **Indexed journals.** The 2003, 2010 and 2011 papers appeared in *Perceptual and Motor Skills*, *Neuroscience Letters* and *Brain Research*.
- **Journal of Consciousness Exploration & Research.** Its pages name the publisher as QuantumDream, Inc. PubMed holds no record from it.
- **SCIRP journals.** The publisher is on Beall's list of potential predatory publishers ([Beall's list](https://beallslist.net/)). PubMed holds no record from the *Journal of Biophysical Chemistry*.
- **NeuroQuantology.** The US National Library of Medicine lists it as not indexed for MEDLINE ([NLM Catalog record](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esummary.fcgi?db=nlmcatalog&id=101234555&retmode=json)).

A weak venue does not make a result wrong. It means the paper has not passed the review mainstream readers rely on, and few independent researchers will see it. Here the largest claims sit in the weakest venues.

### Where the lab's own accounts disagree

- **The sibling study.** The abstract places the effect over "the frontal and occipital lobes" with a "100 msec." configuration. The lab's 2010 retelling says "20+2" and "the (right) parietal lobe" ([Persinger & Lavallee 2010](https://jcer.com/index.php/jcj/article/view/99)). Its 2015 retelling says "the right temporal lobe" ([Scott 2015](https://jcer.com/index.php/jcj/article/view/493)).
- **Burke 2013.** The paper reports 300 km and a field that speeds up (20 to 8 ms). Scott 2015 describes it as "about 400 km" with the effect when the fields ran "in a decelerating manner".
- **The yoked rings.** 15 m apart in one account, 10 m in another.

## The background the authors lean on

### Entanglement

- **What it is.** Two systems prepared together can give measurement results that are correlated more strongly than any shared instruction set allows. Experiments confirm this ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Bell's Theorem](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bell-theorem/)) (**established**).
- **It carries no signal.** The same entry states that "quantum correlations cannot be exploited to send signals superluminally". The correlation shows only when the two records are compared afterwards. A flash at one place that changes an EEG at another would be a signal. So if the lab's effect were real, it would not be entanglement but something unknown to physics. The lab itself wrote that its effect "could be utilized as alternative communication systems" ([Rouleau 2014](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=45572)).
- **How physicists make it.** The lab cites an experiment that entangled two gas samples of about 10¹² caesium atoms with a pulse of light sent through both; the state lasted 0.5 milliseconds ([Julsgaard 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11574882/)). Two coils fed the same signal by one computer share a cause, as two radios tuned to one station do. That is ordinary correlation.
- **Brains.** A physicist's estimate puts the lifetime of quantum coherence in neurons at about 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ seconds, against about 10⁻³ to 10⁻¹ seconds for neural activity ([Tegmark 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11088215/)).

### The earlier "transferred potential" studies

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [Duane & Behrendt 1965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5890891/), *Science* | 15 pairs of identical twins in rooms 6 m apart; one closes the eyes; one EEG channel, read by eye | Alpha rhythm in the other twin in 2 pairs. A 2024 re-analysis of the printed traces found alpha synchrony between a twin and an unrelated person too ([Özkurt 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39439847/)) |
| Puthoff & Targ, SRI, 1974–76 | A strobe flashed at a sender 7 m from a shielded receiver; six receivers | "No evidence of EEG driving in any receiver" at the flash frequency; one receiver showed 24 % less alpha power at 16 flashes a second (P < .04) (Puthoff & Targ 1976; see the reading list) |
| Grinberg-Zylberbaum 1994, *Physics Essays* ([Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.4006/1.3029159)) | Seven pairs in shielded rooms; flashes to one | A "transferred potential" in about one case in four, by a secondary account ([Psi Encyclopedia](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/jacobo-grinberg-zylberbaum/)) |
| [Wackermann 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12493602/), *Neuroscience Letters* | Two shielded rooms; pattern-reversal stimuli to one | EEG "departures" in most unstimulated partners, with "no biophysical mechanism" known |
| [Standish 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15165411/); [Radin 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15165412/) | 30 pairs; 13 pairs | 5 of 60 people significant, and 1 of 4 retested pairs again; r = 0.20, with 3 of 13 pairs significant |
| [Richards 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16398586/); [Achterberg 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16398587/) | The same idea in an fMRI scanner | Positive; but trial order was not randomised, samples were small, and one healer knew the sequence ([Acunzo 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24427128/)) |

Proponents count these as replications of one another; one abstract speaks of "seven laboratories" ([Standish 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15165411/)). They are positive reports from several groups, but each measures something different: alpha in a twin, a drop in alpha, an evoked-potential shape, power fluctuations. Each is **single-lab**. The replication record:
- **The careful repeat failed.** With 17 pairs and Wackermann's protocol, "the EEG power of the non-stimulated subject was not linked to the stimulation of the other subject", and the earlier analysis "turned out to systematically overestimate effects" ([Ambach 2008](https://ejp.wyrdwise.com/EJP%20v23-2.pdf), pp. 131–146).
- **The lead author withdrew.** Wackermann, who led the 2003 study, wrote that "it is highly doubtful that there is anything such as a 'real' effect", and that none of these approaches gave "reproducible results across laboratories" (same issue, pp. 147–153).
- **An fMRI test built to find psi found none.** Related pairs, including twins, gave "indistinguishable neuronal responses" ([Moulton & Kosslyn 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18095790/)).
- **A later positive is small.** With 25 pairs, standard averaging showed nothing in the passive partner and a new algorithm a weak response ([Giroldini 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26966513/)); a re-analysis classified 50.74 % of the EEG correctly, against 50 % by chance ([Bilucaglia 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31497288/)).
- **The reviews.** A systematic review of 143 reports finds "small sample sizes, inadequate multiple-comparison control, and analytical flexibility". Designs like these often reported effects, it says, but the findings were "heterogeneous and seldom replicated" ([Acunzo 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42009267/)). A second, of 92 reports, treats quantum and non-local readings "only as speculative frameworks" ([Batouli 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42456633/)).
- **Preregistered replications.** We found none; the search is necessarily incomplete.

A popular book states more than this record holds: the EEG link "can be ascribed only to nonlocal influence" and was replicated in "three different laboratories" (van Lommel, *Consciousness Beyond Life*, 2010, section "Scientific Proof of the Nonlocal Entanglement of Consciousness") (**claim**). The lab reads such effects as present in "about 10%" of pairs and made reliable by its rotating field ([Scott 2015](https://jcer.com/index.php/jcj/article/view/493)) (**claim**).

## Ordinary explanations to weigh

| Explanation | Where it shows in this record |
|---|---|
| **People who know the timing** can shift a record without meaning to | Burke 2013: the EEG side was told by phone when to mark. Dotta & Persinger 2012: the person at the detector injected by hand and knew which injections were paired |
| **Shared hardware** can carry the stimulus timing | The two rings ran from one computer, and the EEG was recorded inside an active ring. The sources we opened describe no run with a dummy load in place of the head, a control SRI used in the 1970s |
| **Same conditions, similar EEG.** People recorded separately under one schedule show "spurious hyper-connections" ([Burgess 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24399948/)) | Scott 2015: both partners follow one schedule, with the field on at the same minutes |
| **Many comparisons, tiny samples** | Six configurations by bands by lobes (2003); voxel maps in 1-Hz steps (2013); 22 segments, 8 bands and 19 channels (2015); two to five pairs each |
| **No preregistration.** The outcome can be picked afterwards | The effect moved: frontal and occipital theta (2003), right-parietal power with light (2010), single voxels with sound but not light (2013), right-temporal power (2015) |

### What this project cannot test

- **The EEG, light and chemistry claims.** They need EEG or laboratory detectors, which the project does not provide.

### What a fair test with two helmets would need

This is a design for a laboratory with EEG and ethics approval, not a home recipe.

1. **Two informed volunteers in two rooms**, with no cable or mains circuit joining the stimulus side to the recording side.
2. **A random schedule of flash and no-flash periods**, drawn by a computer, committed in advance and unknown to both experimenters. The stimulus side runs by itself.
3. **Real or sham field for both helmets**, chosen at random for each session and hidden from everyone present.
4. **Continuous EEG without markers.** A third person aligns the schedule only after the recording is locked.
5. **One preregistered outcome**, for example power at the flash frequency at one named electrode, flash against no-flash, real against sham, with the test and the number of pairs fixed in advance and every result reported.
6. **A dummy run** of the whole schedule with a saline phantom in place of the head, to catch electrical pickup.

### With one helmet: the telepathy trial

The telepathy trial asks the question that matters, whether any information gets across, with an outcome anyone can score ([Telepathy](EFFECT_TELEPATHY.md)).
- In [Trials](#trials), choose mode **Telepathy** and the `telepathy-sender` program, and tick **Blind**. The sender, in another room, sees the target on a second phone; the receiver writes, locks the response and ranks the pictures blind ([EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md), "Telepathy trials").
- [Shapes and colours telepathy test (two people)](#guides/shapes-colors) is a simpler version; [Helmet real vs sham with a psi task](#guides/helmet-factorial) compares the two arms.
- With 5 pictures, a true hit rate of 30 % against 20 % needs 127 trials. The helmet's part shows only as real better than sham.

Read [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) first. These programs are not for anyone with epilepsy or a seizure history; stop on headache, nausea or distress. Both partners must know about the experiment and agree to it.
