# Remote viewing: what it is, why it is tied to the God Helmet, and how to test it

Remote viewing is the claimed ability to describe a hidden target (a picture, a place or a future event) without any normal sensory route to it. It is one of the effects people most often link with the God Helmet. Michael Persinger's laboratory tested the former government remote viewer Ingo Swann and the psychic Sean Harribance with weak magnetic fields, and Todd Murphy sells Shakti and Shiva sessions designed for psi. The US government paid for remote-viewing research from 1972 to 1995.

This article sets out how remote viewing sessions work, what the God Helmet literature claims, what the government programmes found, where the scientific debate stands, and how to run an honest trial with this helmet. The short version: laboratory remote viewing produced effects that critics and supporters agree are hard to explain as chance, but they come mostly from one research group, were never independently replicated under tight controls, and the God Helmet link rests on one-subject studies from one lab.

## How to read this article

The evidence words are the ones used across the project:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **established** | Independently replicated |
| **single-lab** | Reported by one laboratory or research group only |
| **claim** | Stated by an author, agency or vendor without independent support |
| **anecdote** | A personal account |
| **unverified** | Could not be checked against a primary source |

Nothing about remote viewing itself is **established** in this sense. The word applies only to some negative findings, such as the inconsistent judging in ARV and the absence of a field effect in independent God Helmet tests.

Military and intelligence material is described as documentary history only. Nothing here is guidance for observing or influencing other people.

## What remote viewing is

The term was coined at the Stanford Research Institute (SRI) in the early 1970s, where the physicists Russell Targ and Harold Puthoff tested Ingo Swann, Pat Price, Hella Hammid and others. Later protocols differ in how the target is chosen, how the viewer responds and how the response is scored. They all share three parts:

1. **A hidden target**, chosen at random after the viewer has been isolated, so that no one with the viewer knows it.
2. **A free response**: the viewer talks, writes and sketches impressions, usually for 15 to 30 minutes.
3. **Blind judging**: someone who does not know the target compares the response with the real target and several decoys.

The third part is what makes it a test. Any description matches some picture loosely; the question is whether it matches the real target better than the decoys more often than chance.

### Protocols in brief

| Protocol | How it works | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| **Outbound (beacon) viewing** | A "beacon" person drives to a randomly drawn local site and stays there for a fixed time. The viewer, with an experimenter who does not know the site, describes it. Judges later visit all sites in the pool and rank the transcripts | SRI's first published series ([Targ & Puthoff 1974](https://www.nature.com/articles/251602a0); [Puthoff 1996](https://newdualism.org/papers/H.Puthoff/CIA-Initiated%20Remote%20Viewing%20At%20Stanford%20Research%20Institute.htm)) |
| **Coordinate remote viewing (CRV)** | The target is given only as a reference number (originally geographic coordinates). The viewer works through a staged written structure, from a quick pen mark to sensory words, sketches and a summary | Developed by Ingo Swann at SRI for the Army from about 1982; codified in 1983–86 ([DIA SUN STREAK briefing](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB534-DIA-Declassified-Sourcebook/documents/DIA-21.pdf)) |
| **Extended remote viewing (ERV)** | Long sessions in deep relaxation, often lying down in a darkened room, aimed at "being at" the site | Army and DIA unit practice, built on the observation that viewing seemed "facilitated by physical relaxation and enhanced internal attention" ([DIA SUN STREAK briefing](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB534-DIA-Declassified-Sourcebook/documents/DIA-21.pdf)) |
| **Photo-target free response** | The target is one picture from a sealed set; the viewer describes the picture they will see as feedback | SAIC's standard laboratory design in the 1990s ([Utts 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/air)) |
| **Associative remote viewing (ARV)** | Two very different pictures are mapped to two outcomes of a future event. The viewer describes the picture they will be shown after the outcome; the better match is the prediction | Targ, Puthoff and Keith Harary in the early 1980s ([Katz et al. 2021](https://www.koestler-parapsychology.psy.ed.ac.uk/Documents/KPU_1034_Published_Results.pdf)) |

**CRV in general terms.** The declassified 1985 working paper splits a session into six stages: a first spontaneous pen mark and a gut reaction; sensory words (colours, textures, temperatures, sounds); dimensions and sketches; a matrix that separates kinds of data; queries on single words; and three-dimensional modelling ([CRV working paper 1985](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001000400001-7.pdf)). Its core idea is to keep raw impressions apart from named guesses, which it calls "analytic overlay". [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) gives the stage table and the session classes. No controlled study shows that the CRV structure improves accuracy (**claim**).

**Judging and scoring.** Two methods dominate:
- **Ranking.** The judge ranks every picture in the set against the response, 1 being the best match. A "hit" is the target ranked first; with 5 pictures, chance is 20 %. The mean rank and a standardised rank score use all the information.
- **Rating.** The judge scores the match on a fixed scale (for example 0–7), or scores each element of the response against a list of target features. SRI's 1983 "fuzzy set" form is an example; it was not validated ([SRI 1983](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001800080001-5.pdf)).

**Effect size.** Utts's figure, used throughout this article, is the standardised rank score per trial. With 5-picture sets, an effect size of 0.2 corresponds to a mean target rank of about 2.72 instead of the chance value of 3 ([Utts 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/air)). It is a small effect: most sessions are not hits.

## Why it is linked to the God Helmet

### Persinger's work with Ingo Swann (1998–2002)

Ingo Swann, an artist and the best-known SRI subject, visited Persinger's laboratory at Laurentian University in August 1998. Two papers followed.

- **Swann under rotating fields.** Swann received one 30-minute exposure to circumcerebral magnetic fields: eight solenoids in a ring around the head, the field stepping counter-clockwise, in six 5-minute configurations (three burst-firing, three frequency-modulated "Thomas" patterns, about 2–5 µT). The abstract says the fields "significantly altered his subjective experiences". In remote-viewing trials on the following days, the proportion of unusual 7 Hz spike-and-slow-wave EEG activity over the occipital lobes correlated with rated accuracy (rho = .50). MRI and neuropsychological tests showed an unusual right parieto-occipital organisation ([Persinger, Roll, Tiller, Koren & Cook 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12081299/)). One subject, no sham session, and the accuracy ratings and EEG came from the same team (**single-lab**).
- **Fields around the target.** In a pilot, the sealed target photographs in another room were surrounded by weak fields (under 20 nT over most of the area). Patterns generated under Windows, which the authors say phase-modulated the waveform, were followed by a marked fall in Swann's accuracy; the same nominal patterns generated under DOS were not ([Koren & Persinger 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12509207/)). This is a pilot with one subject (**single-lab**). It is also the origin of the lab's claim that timing jitter destroys the effect, which became central to the later replication dispute ([HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)).

In a public lecture Persinger presented Swann as someone who "helped develop the phenomenon of remote viewing" ([3pdDtH4CvTY at 8:32](https://youtu.be/3pdDtH4CvTY?t=512)) and described the target photographs sitting in envelopes in another room ([12:57](https://youtu.be/3pdDtH4CvTY?t=777)).

### Persinger and Sean Harribance

Sean Harribance, a psychic, reported lifelong "flashes of images" of hidden objects and of personal details of strangers.
- **2002 SPECT study.** The specificity of his descriptions of target pictures of people correlated with the proportion of occipital alpha activity. SPECT and EEG suggested more activity in the right parietal and occipital regions. Weak fields patterned like hippocampal long-term potentiation (LTP), applied over his right temporoparietal region, evoked sensed presences on his left side ([Roll, Persinger, Webster, Tiller & Cook 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12325407/)) (**single-lab**, one subject).
- **2010 retelling.** Persinger later reported 22 presence or vision button presses during the LTP pattern against 5–12 for other patterns, with a 6:1 left-side bias. A 20-second rotation of the same pattern lowered his alpha rhythm to 25–30 % of baseline and his rated accuracy by about 20 % ([Persinger 2010](http://web.archive.org/web/20150426091303id_/http://www.neuroquantology.com/index.php/journal/article/download/301/346)). The number and length of trials are not given (**single-lab**).
- **EEG signature.** A later sLORETA analysis of his recorded EEG found 20–30 Hz power in the right parahippocampal region during readings ([Persinger & Saroka 2012](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3410194/fullTextXML)). Again one subject.

Note that in the Harribance work the fields mostly *lowered* accuracy or produced presences; none of these papers shows fields improving psi performance.

### Geomagnetic and sidereal-time claims

Persinger argued for decades that psi experiences and performance track geomagnetic activity. In the same lecture he says such experiences usually happen "when the geomagnetic activity is less intense" ([3pdDtH4CvTY at 5:41](https://youtu.be/3pdDtH4CvTY?t=341)).
- **Dream ESP.** Persinger and Krippner (1989) reported that the Maimonides dream sessions scored best on geomagnetically quiet nights. We have seen this only in secondary sources (**unverified**) ([Psi Encyclopedia: Persinger](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/michael-persinger)).
- **Inside the programme.** A 1984 study for the Army correlated scored sessions with solar, geomagnetic and local ELF data and cited Persinger's papers as part of its rationale. It called itself a pilot ([Adams & Puthoff 1984](https://documents2.theblackvault.com/documents/cia/stargate/STARGATE%20%234%20176/Part0003/CIA-RDP96-00788R001800270001-4.pdf)) (**single-lab**).
- **Sidereal time.** Spottiswoode reported much larger effect sizes near 13.5 h local sidereal time in a post-hoc analysis of free-response trials (Spottiswoode 1997, *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 11(2)). No independent replication is known (**single-lab**).

The app logs Kp, ap and local sidereal time with every trial as exploratory variables. Do not schedule sessions by them ([RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)).

### Murphy's Shakti and Shiva psi sessions

Todd Murphy, who sells the 8-coil Shakti and the Shiva replica of Persinger's "Octopus" ring, describes psi configurations of his own.
- **The chirp ring.** In his lecture on psychic skills, Murphy describes a counter-clockwise chirp signal around the ring with the time over each coil growing from 10 to 20 ms, and places psi in the right hemisphere ([tmC1174POpA](https://youtu.be/tmC1174POpA); notes in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)) (**claim**).
- **Shakti remote-viewing design.** His Shakti pages propose a modulated 40 Hz signal over the occipital lobes, citing the occipital 7 Hz finding in Swann. The page itself says this signal "has not been tested in practice for enhancement of remote viewing skills" ([Murphy: Shakti remote viewing](https://www.god-helmet.com/wp/winshakti/demo/shakti_demo/remote_v.htm)) (**unverified**).
- **Telepathy design.** A second page proposes the same signal over the frontal lobes, then the occipital lobes ([Murphy: psychic skills](https://www.god-helmet.com/wp/winshakti/demo/shakti_demo/psychic.htm)), and states that none of its procedures has been tested (**unverified**).

### What the independent evidence says

No laboratory outside Persinger's has tested whether weak rotating fields change remote-viewing accuracy. Independent double-blind tests of God-Helmet-type fields on experience found no field effect: what people reported tracked suggestibility and absorption, not the field ([Granqvist et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15849873/); [Gendle & McGrath 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22582690/)). The God Helmet connection to remote viewing therefore rests on two one-subject papers from one lab, plus vendor designs that the vendor says are untested.

## The scientific debate

### The early SRI work and Marks & Kammann

Targ and Puthoff published their first results in *Nature* in 1974, with an editorial explaining why the journal printed a paper its referees had doubts about ([Targ & Puthoff 1974](https://www.nature.com/articles/251602a0)). The psychologists David Marks and Richard Kammann tried to repeat the outbound experiments in New Zealand and failed. Looking at SRI's transcripts, they found cues a judge could use to put them in order without any psi: references to dates and to earlier sessions. Because the sessions ran in a known order, such cues could let a judge match transcripts to sites. Their repeats "do not verify" SRI's conclusions ([Marks & Kammann 1978](https://www.nature.com/articles/274680a0)). Tart, Puthoff and Targ re-judged edited transcripts and reported that the result survived (*Nature* 284:191, 1980); Marks and Kammann were not persuaded. The episode set the standard rule: clean transcripts, random target order and judges who know nothing about the sequence.

### Utts versus Hyman (1995)

The AIR review commissioned two outside assessments.
- **Jessica Utts**, a statistician, concluded that psychic functioning "has been well established". She reported effect sizes of 0.209 over 770 SRI sessions and 0.230 over 455 SAIC sessions, and argued that the consistency across laboratories (including ganzfeld work) ruled out chance ([Utts 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/air)).
- **Ray Hyman**, a psychologist, agreed the SAIC effects were "too large and consistent to be dismissed as statistical flukes". He argued that this does not show a paranormal cause: one person, Edwin May, was the sole judge of all the SAIC free-response experiments; the same small group of viewers and targets was reused; and a real phenomenon would need boundary conditions and independent replication with new viewers, pools and judges ([Hyman 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/hyman.html)).

Their disagreement is less about the numbers than about what would count as proof. Both accepted that the lab data were not chance. Utts weighed the consistency; Hyman weighed the dependence on one group and the lack of a theory that predicts when the effect should appear. Later re-examinations sided with the call for caution: Wiseman and Milton found possible leakage routes in SAIC's Experiment One, which AIR had used as its worked example, and the SAIC team gave several different accounts of its procedure when asked ([Wiseman & Milton 1998](https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/2331/); [reply to May 1999](https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/experiment-one-of-the-saic-remote-viewing-program-a-critical-re-e-2/)).

### Known weak points

| Problem | What happened | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensory leakage** | Cues reached judges or viewers through transcripts, experimenters or the room | Documented in the SRI transcripts ([Marks & Kammann 1978](https://www.nature.com/articles/274680a0)); possible in SAIC Experiment One ([Wiseman & Milton 1998](https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/2331/)) |
| **Judging cues and single judges** | One judge for all SAIC free-response work; ARV taskers who knew both pictures among the judges | [Hyman 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/hyman.html); [Smith, Laham & Moddel 2010](https://www.colorado.edu/faculty/moddel/sites/default/files/attached-files/smith_sse10_abstract.pdf) |
| **Target reuse and pool design** | Early SRI sites drawn without replacement; reused targets scored higher in the ganzfeld; SAIC video targets scored zero until the pool was rebuilt | [Utts 1995](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~jutts/air); [Hyman 1994](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/remoteviewing/stargate/STARGATE%20%237%20179/Part0001/CIA-RDP96-00789R003200100001-5.TXT); [Lantz, Luke & May 1994](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/remoteviewing/stargate/STARGATE%20%231%20173/Part0003/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200100002-4.TXT) |
| **File drawer and flexible analysis** | Unreported series and post-hoc analyses inflate pooled results | In the ganzfeld, modelled questionable practices could explain about 60 % of the effect ([Bierman, Spottiswoode & Bijl 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153049)) |
| **Operational claims** | Famous "hits" are selected from many sessions; misses are rarely counted | AIR found operational value unsupported ([AIR 1995](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4.pdf)) |

### Meta-analyses and replications since 1995

- **A proponent meta-analysis.** Tressoldi and Katz pooled 36 remote-viewing studies (40 effect sizes) from 1974 to 2022. They report an average effect size of .34 after excluding outliers, no signs of publication bias and a minimal decline, and conclude that remote viewing outperforms other ESP protocols ([Tressoldi & Katz 2023](https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/view/2931)) (**claim**; the pooled studies are mostly unregistered, and many come from a few groups).
- **A pre-registered test.** In a pre-registered study at the Koestler Parapsychology Unit, 44 experienced remote viewers produced 10 correct matches (23 %, chance 25 %) ([Lyke, KPU 1055](https://www.koestler-parapsychology.psy.ed.ac.uk/Documents/KPU_1055_Results.pdf)) (**single-lab**, null).
- **ARV re-judged.** When nine new judges re-scored 86 completed ARV trials, all nine agreed on only 6.9 % of them, and the new judges averaged 51.5 % correct against 65 % for the original judges. An applied currency-trading project lost $52,186 ([Katz et al. 2021](https://www.koestler-parapsychology.psy.ed.ac.uk/Documents/KPU_1034_Published_Results.pdf)). The earlier positive ARV series, such as Targ's 1982 silver-futures forecasts (9 of 9, **claim**) and 7 of 7 Dow Jones predictions by students ([Smith, Laham & Moddel 2010](https://www.colorado.edu/faculty/moddel/sites/default/files/attached-files/smith_sse10_abstract.pdf)), were small and not judged blind.
- **Pre-registration in general.** In the Koestler Unit's registry, 16 % of pre-registered confirmatory hypotheses were supported, against 46.6 % in non-registered papers of the same period ([Wiseman & Watt 2026](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.21360)). A large pre-registered multi-lab replication of Bem's precognition design found 49.89 % against 50 % chance over 37,836 trials ([Kekecs et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.191375)).
- **The wider argument.** A 2018 review in *American Psychologist* argued that the psi evidence is comparable to that for accepted psychological effects ([Cardeña 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000236)); a reply argued that the claims conflict with established physics and cannot be right ([Reber & Alcock 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000486)).

**Where this leaves things.** The SRI/SAIC laboratory effect is **single-lab**. Independent, pre-registered tests with experienced viewers have not reproduced it. ARV judging is unreliable (**established** as a methodological finding). No brain marker of remote viewing has been validated; SAIC's own MEG and EEG searches found nothing ([AIR 1995](https://documents3.theblackvault.com/documents/stargate/pdfs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4.pdf); [BRAIN_VIEW.md](BRAIN_VIEW.md)).

### Running a proper trial

[EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) has the full method. The essentials:

1. **Pre-register.** Before the first trial, write down the hypothesis, the one primary outcome (for example first-place hits against chance, or real against sham), the number of trials, the set size and who judges. Date it and, if you can, post it publicly.
2. **Have someone else build the picture pool** in [Trials → Picture pool](#trials/pictures): sets of 4 or 5 pictures of the same size, as different as possible within a set, many sets drawn with replacement. Import it without looking (never press **Show pictures** yourself) and note the pool fingerprint.
3. **Create the trial** in [Trials](#trials): mode "Remote viewing", a stimulation program, **Blind** ticked. The helmet's hardware random generator picks the set, the target and real or sham, and shows a SHA-256 commitment before you start.
4. **Respond** during or after the stimulation: shapes, colours, textures, sketches; raw impressions rather than names. Then **lock** the response.
5. **Judge blind.** Better than judging yourself: hand the phone to someone who does not know the target, the condition or the viewer. Remove dates and references to other trials from the response first. Every picture gets a different rank.
6. **Reveal.** The app checks the commitment, the pool and the locked response. Aborted trials stay in the list.
7. **Analyse once**, at the planned number of trials. Everything other than your primary outcome (Kp, sidereal time, per-program results) is exploratory.

### How many trials

From [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md), for 80 % power at one-sided α = .05:

| Design | Chance | Assumed true rate | Trials needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 pictures, first-place hits | 20 % | 30 % | 127 |
| 5 pictures, rank score | ES 0 | ES 0.2 (the SRI/SAIC level) | about 155 |
| 4 pictures, first-place hits | 25 % | 32 % | 279 |
| ARV, non-pass predictions | 50 % | 60 % | 169 |

Testing the helmet's contribution (real against sham) needs more still, because you are looking for a difference between two small effects. A personal series of 20 or 30 trials is a pilot: at the SRI/SAIC level it will usually not reach significance even if the effect is real, and a "significant" short series is often luck.

### Honest expectations

- **Expect chance.** The best-controlled recent tests of experienced viewers found chance results. Most people's series will look like chance.
- **Impressions are not evidence.** Vivid imagery is common with relaxation, sensory reduction and suggestion, with or without a field. Only blind ranking against decoys counts.
- **The helmet arm is the real question.** If you see an effect, the informative test is whether it differs between real and sham sessions that you could not tell apart. Record your guess of the condition after each session.
- **Report everything.** Nulls are useful. Export the raw trial file and keep it with your pre-registration.
